### Why most feedback fails

[00:00] okay so today I want to talk about feedback and specifically why most of the feedback we give just doesn't change anything. you've probably had this happen, you tell someone something about their work, they nod, and then next week it's exactly the same

[00:41] and the reason usually isn't that the person didn't care. it's that the feedback was too vague to act on. "this presentation was a bit flat" or "be more proactive". the person hears that something is wrong but they have no idea what to do differently on Monday

[01:20] the second reason is that feedback often arrives as a threat. when people feel judged their attention goes to defending themselves, not to the content. so you can say something completely true and useful and it bounces off

### Describe what happened, not who they are

[02:05] so the first fix is to describe a specific behaviour, in a specific situation, and the impact it had. one way to remember it is situation, behaviour, impact. "in Tuesday's client call, when you jumped in before they finished their question, they stopped explaining the problem and we never heard the real issue"

[02:52] notice what that does. it's about something that happened, not about the person's character. "you interrupted in that call" is something you can change. "you're a bad listener" is an identity, and people will fight you on their identity

[03:30] and the impact part matters because it gives the reason. without the impact the person just hears your preference. with it they can see why it matters, and they can even find a better fix than the one you had in mind

### Ask before you tell

[04:10] second fix. before you give your view, ask for theirs. "how do you think the call went?" a lot of the time the person already noticed the thing you were going to say, and if they say it themselves they own it, it isn't something you did to them

[04:48] and if they didn't notice it, their answer tells you where they're starting from, so you know how much to explain

### Timing and the sandwich

[05:25] third, timing. feedback works best close to the event, while the person still remembers the details. same day or next day is ideal. if you save it all up for a quarterly review, the details are gone and it feels like an ambush

[06:02] now what about the feedback sandwich, praise, criticism, praise. I'd skip it. people learn the pattern very quickly and then they discount the praise because they're waiting for the "but". keep praise and criticism separate, and make the praise just as specific as the criticism, so it tells them what to keep doing

### End with a next step

[06:50] last thing. end with one concrete next step that you agree on together, not a list of ten things. one change they can try in the next week. and then follow up and notice when they do it

[07:25] so to recap, be specific about the situation, the behaviour and the impact, ask for their view first, give it soon, skip the sandwich, and agree one next step. thanks for watching
