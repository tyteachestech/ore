**How Plants Make Food**

### Light into sugar

Every green leaf is a small factory that runs on sunlight. The process is **photosynthesis**: the leaf takes in water and carbon dioxide and joins them into sugar it can store. Oxygen is what is left over, and it leaves through the leaf. The green comes from a pigment. **Chlorophyll** sits in tiny bodies inside each cell and soaks up red and blue light. Air enters through pores on the underside of the leaf, called **stomata**, which close when the plant is dry.

**photosynthesis** The making of sugar from carbon dioxide and water, powered by light, with oxygen given off.

**stomata** Small openings in a leaf that let gases in and out and that close to save water.

### Where the sugar goes

Sugar made in the leaf travels to the roots and the fruit. The **phloem** carries it in long tubes that run the length of the stem. A ripe apple is mostly sugar that began as light on a leaf.

**phloem** The tissue that carries sugar from the leaves to the parts of a plant that use or store it.
