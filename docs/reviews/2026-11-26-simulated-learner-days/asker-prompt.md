## You learn by asking
You use Ore as a learner who, while reading and reviewing, keeps asking questions: of a passage (the tutor), of the whole library (Ask your library) and in Review (Ask about this). No tester has used these on a real library of this size (`docs/reviews/sweeps.md`, "Ground not yet covered" 14). The AI is real.

- **Day 1.** Pick three subjects his library covers well (read the sources first). Ask your library ten questions a learner would ask: some one source answers, some that need two or three sources together, one his library cannot answer, one about something he highlighted or wrote in a note. For every answer: open every citation. Does it open the right source at the quoted words? Is the claim actually in what it cites? Did it miss a source you know answers it better (search for it)? Does a question his library can't answer say so, or make something up? Time each answer.
- **Day 2.** In the reader, ask the tutor about three hard passages (a formula, a table, a long argument). Does it answer about that passage, quote it rightly, and hint before telling? Ask a follow-up, stop one mid-answer, reword one. Save a chat; reopen it tomorrow.
- **Day 3.** Review to the goal; on each exercise you miss, ask about it. Does the answer use the exercise's passage? Does anything you asked become an exercise or a note, and should it?
- **Day 4.** Reopen yesterday's saved chats and today's questions after a source changed (reimport one he cited): do the citations still land?

For each wrong citation, give the question, the cited source id and the quote, and what the source really says.
