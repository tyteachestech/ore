Effect sizes are Cohen's d as printed. The distilled/claims files were not used.

Convention in this file: **Book:** = what the text says. **Inference:** = my reading for Ore. Adult, self-directed reading of expository non-fiction is not the population of most studies (mostly school and college, short passages, immediate or one-week tests). The book itself says so (ch10 l132).

- Self-explaining median: d = 0.61 from 54 comparisons (ch07 l5, l18, l114; ch10 l5, Table 10.1 l38). The ch07 conclusion says 0.68 from 54 (l280), and ch10 says 0.68 from 45 (l48). Use **0.61, 44 of 54**.

- Berthold, Eysink & Renkl 2009: the prose (ch07 l202) and Table 7.1 (l134–137) swap the open and assisted numbers. Both agree that assisted prompts gave **d = 1.16 on conceptual knowledge**.
- Chiou 2009 individual mapping: blank in Table 3.4 (l193), d = 0.90 in the text (l236).

## 2. Decision-by-decision verdicts

### D15: highlight intents and per-intent placeholders. **Needs a tweak** (Card placeholder)

- **Book:** Self-explanation means going beyond the text; repeating or paraphrasing is not self-explanation (ch07 l84). Generic "type comments to yourself" gave 0.11 (l242). Focused or specific prompts beat general ones (l216, l270). Explaining what a passage means and how it relates to prior knowledge beat "why"-only elaborative interrogation (O'Reilly; l188). ch10 suggests annotating highlights with the names of related ideas (l74; a suggestion, not tested). Self-questioning with generic question stems, after training, gave 0.67 and 1.03 (King 1992; ch07 l184). A wrong pretest guess still improved later learning (Richland; ch06 l252).
- **Inference:**
  - "This works because…" is a causal, focused stem and is supported.
  - "In my words…" is a paraphrase or summary stem. Paraphrase is not harmful: it matched summarizing in Bretzing & Kulhavy (ch02 l150). But it is the general, knowledge-telling form, and D32 now covers summarizing.
  - So the Card placeholder should pair the causal stem with a prior-knowledge link, not with a paraphrase.
  - Question "My question… my guess:" is supported: a self-question plus a prediction.
  - Keep and Research "In my words…" are low-stakes and produce no items, so leave them.
  - Anchoring the note to a selected passage already makes the prompt specific, which is a plus.

### D18: prequestions, closed-book finish, free recall, AI feedback, use/dispute line, EPUB chapter recall. **Supported, with one gap** (central ideas are retrieved only once)

**Prequestions: supported.**
- **Book:** pretesting helped even on items answered wrong (ch06 l252). Pre-lesson quizzing gave little benefit in one classroom study (l228).
- **Inference:** keep the prequestions few and broad, as D18 already does.

**Closed-book re-ask and free recall: supported.**
- **Book:** generative formats beat recognition (l262). 3R beat rereading (l256). Free recall beat mapping with the text open (l258). Expecting a closed-book test raises effort (l244).

**AI feedback after the attempt: supported.**
- **Book:** feedback is needed, especially when recall is poor (l212–214, l262). Immediate and delayed feedback both work (l218). Showing provided explanations first cancels generation (ch07 l200).

**EPUB per-chapter recall: supported.**
- **Book:** summarizing and testing evidence comes from short passages (ch02 l190). Repeated retrieval beats a single test (ch06 l246).

**"Where would you use or dispute this?": not addressed by the book.** It is consistent with:
- transfer-appropriate processing: a practice transfer test helped later transfer (Johnson & Mayer; ch06 l220);
- the finding that summaries alone hurt evaluation (Annis; ch02 l154).

**Gap.**
- **Book:**
  - The testing benefit mostly applies to tested items (ch06 l202, l224, l264).
  - Repeated tests beat one test (l246).
  - A practice test two days later was far stronger than an immediate one: 3.05 vs −0.10 (l206).
- **Inference:**
  - The finish recall happens once, immediately after reading.
  - The central ideas then come back only if Tyler happens to put them in My notes, which then become items.
  - The ideas he missed at the finish are the least likely to reach My notes. See should-change 1.

### D19: items only from Card highlights and My notes, credited "from your notes". **Needs a tweak** (add the prequestions as prompts)

- **Book:** no study on who authors flashcards. The relevant points:
  - Practice should match how the knowledge will be used (ch06 l270).
  - The testing effect is limited to tested items (l264).
  - Summarizing's benefit comes from producing the summary (ch02 l84–88), not from keeping it.
- **Inference:**
  - Drafting items from his own recall or summary is sound for *what* is phrased: the items use his own selection and wording. It is biased in *what* is covered, because it covers what he already retrieved.
  - Keep/Question/Research producing no items fits the book's low rating of highlighting (ch10 l72).

### D20: AI drafts; first review is the finish step (answer cold, see draft, edit). **Supported**

- **Book:**
  - Generate first, then compare with the expert version and revise. This is the "supported" drawing condition: 0.94 vs 0.37 unsupported (Van Meter; ch04 l170–172). It is also slow and should be used sparingly (l200).
  - Testing followed by feedback (ch06 l262).
  - Showing the explanation first cancels the benefit (ch07 l200).
- **Inference:** D20 has the right order. The cost is review time, which D20 already limits to the first appearance.

### D31: per-source My notes seeded by the recall text, refined with the source open. **Supported**

- **Book:**
  - Closed-book summarizing vs open-book summarizing is unresolved. If retrieval fails, give feedback, and the learner may refer back after writing (ch02 l200). That is exactly retrieve, then feedback, then refine with the text.
  - 3R (recite, then reread) beat rereading (ch06 l256).
  - Summarizing notes after a lecture beat reviewing them (ch02 l182).
- **Inference:** the order is right. The book adds a caution: a summary is not enough for synthesis or evaluation (Annis; ch02 l154). The use/dispute line and the application prompts (D34.9) should stay alongside it.

### D32: optional one-line "main point of this section, in your words" at each heading. **Supported in form; needs a tweak for quality**

**(a) One line is enough.**
- **Book:** one-sentence summaries after every paragraph produced the largest summary effects in the book: 1.58 and 0.99 (Doctorow; ch02 l148). Frequent summaries work when they are short (l190). Dilution showed up with several fuller summaries of one short text: 0.21 vs 0.86 (Spurlin), 0.08 vs 0.42 (Foos) (l156, l162).
- **Inference:** the length and frequency in D32 are fine.

**Training and quality matter more than length.**
- **Book:** an untrained replication failed because students wrote inaccurate summaries (Hooper; l160). College students also struggle (l204). Effective training used explicit rules, modeling, and practice with feedback (l172, l180) or comparing summaries with peers (l174). The book recommends direct instruction first (l198).

**(b) Text visible is correct for this step.**
- **Book:** the ch02 evidence is for summaries written with the material available (ch02 l80).
- **Inference:** keeping the text open for section lines and closed for the finish is consistent with the book.

**Limits.**
- **Book:** verbal summaries hurt on spatial or science-diagram content (−0.40; l164).
- **Inference:** the one-key dismiss already covers diagram-heavy sections.

### D33: deferred multi-highlight notes. **Supported (deferral); not directly addressed**

- **Book:** linking ideas across passages is organizing, which is mapping's job.
  - Mapping helps low-ability learners and does little for high-ability ones (ch03 l274).
  - It costs time (l284).
  - Several organizer types at once overwhelmed learners (l270).
  - Outlining matched mapping (l226).
  - ch10 suggests cheap highlight annotations naming related ideas (l74).
- **Inference:**
  - Deferring is right.
  - If revisited, the book favors a *constrained* structure (a matrix, or a few link types such as "evidence for", "leads to", "type of"; ch03 l140) over free-form linking.
  - The strongest, cheapest form is a matrix for compare-and-contrast material (see should-change 5).

### Point (e): mapping, matrices and "provided structure"

- **Book:**
  - Matrix organizers: d = 1.07, 8 of 8, college students, filled in while reading, on screen (Kauffman, Jairam & Kiewra, Ponce & Mayer; ch03 l264–268).
  - The shells were provided: the topics across the top and the categories down the side.
  - Limits: compare-and-contrast texts only (l270); free concept maps add little for high-ability learners (l274); retrieval beats mapping (ch06 l258).
- **Inference:**
  - Ore currently provides no structure inside My notes. A Tyler-specific signal points the same way: his best NotionRPG synthesis was a matrix (Definition / Why it works / Evil brother; brief-notetaking.md "What NotionRPG got right").

### Point (g): learning by teaching vs "written explaining for an imagined audience adds little"

- **Book:**
  - Every Table 8.1 study is oral, video or peer explaining; the book reports no written-for-an-audience condition.
  - Video explanation to an imagined student beat studying and preparing to teach at a delay (0.79, 0.70; time-equated 0.56, 0.90). It beat a practice recall test (0.55) (ch08 l144).
  - The same format lost badly to self-explaining (−1.63) when tutors fell into knowledge telling (Roscoe & Chi; l138).
  - The active ingredient the book names is reflective knowledge building, prompted by deep questions (l158).
- **Inference:**
  - The brief's "speaking does" is too strong. The book does not settle written vs spoken explaining.
  - What it shows is that the audience matters less than whether the explanation monitors understanding and draws inferences.
  - The brief's conclusion for Ore (no "explain to a novice" frame in writing) still holds.
  - The book adds a cheap mechanism Ore already half has: one deep follow-up question after an attempt.

## 3. Should change (smallest concrete change each)

1. **D19 + D18: make the prequestions review items.** After the finish step, each of the 2–4 prequestions becomes a `prompt` item. Its rubric is the key points from the AI feedback, and Tyler finishes it in the first review as in D20.
   - **Book:** the testing benefit is mostly limited to tested items (ch06 l202, l224, l264). Repeated tests beat one (l246). Delayed practice beats immediate (l206). Feedback matters most when recall was poor (l262).
   - **Inference:** today the central ideas he missed at the finish never come back unless he writes them into My notes himself. This adds at most 4 items per source.
2. **D15: change the Card placeholder** from "In my words… / This works because…" to "This works because… / Connects to…". Keep, Research and Question stay as they are.
   - **Book:** self-explanation goes beyond paraphrase (ch07 l84). Linking to prior knowledge beat "why"-only (l188). Generic comments gave 0.11 (l242). Specific wording is better (l270). ch10 suggests naming related ideas on highlights (l74).
   - **Inference:** summarizing now lives in D32, so the Card note should carry the integrating half.
3. **D32: add practice with feedback, not a tutorial.** The chapter-recall and finish feedback (already shown after the attempt) adds one line on his section lines this time: whether they state a main idea or a detail, and whether they are his words or the author's. Length and frequency stay as they are.
   - **Book:** summary quality decides the effect (ch02 l160, l170). Effective training used practice with feedback or comparison (l174, l180). College students also struggle (l204).
   - **Inference:** this is the smallest form of "training" that adds no screen and no AI text to note fields (D34.2 and D34.3 still hold).
4. **Docs: correct the teaching claim** in `docs/research/brief-notetaking-practice.md` (§1.8 failure modes, l208, and principle 13, l435–436).    - No product change.
5. **D34.2 (lower priority, an n-of-1 candidate): allow empty structure, never text.** For a chapter or source with a compare-and-contrast shape, My notes may offer, on one key, an empty table: the compared items as columns and the dimensions as rows, with all cells blank for Tyler.
