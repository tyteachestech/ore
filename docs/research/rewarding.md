# What makes Ore rewarding

> Research, 2026-11-11. Full page with every source link: https://claude.ai/artifact/3P9EVZ8v71jE4rZaovJVr1. **Outcome:** Tyler picked mechanics 1 to 3 and measure A, built as D197. "Set in a tool" (the last gem grade) waits for real tries (D148.3). Evidence was read from abstracts, except Lally 2009 (full text).

Tyler asked what would make Ore as rewarding and motivating as possible, as specific mechanics and measures, not habit advice. Ore already covers competence, autonomy and good endings (D62, D90, D177, D190); the gaps were that a miss only read as damage, nothing showed progress past recall, and the cut count stands still for weeks.

## Mechanics

| # | Mechanic | What he sees | Why it should work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Gold seam** (built) | A cracked gem he wins back is mended in gold and keeps it | Errors corrected are the best remembered (Butterfield & Metcalfe 2001) |
| 2 | **Gem grades** (built to polished) | Cut, then polished when passed on a new case (a remix), then set in a tool when used in a real try | Testing transfers to application, d = 0.40 (Pan & Rickard 2018) |
| 3 | **Richer ore for harder recalls** (built) | Bigger, brighter ore for a recall that had nearly faded | Rewarding effort itself made people choose harder tasks later (Clay 2022); effort adds value (Inzlicht 2017) |
| 4 | Teach the mole | The mole asks one Explain a session and keeps what he taught it | Pupils worked harder for an agent they taught (Chase 2009, 8th graders) |
| 5 | Geodes | A new exercise is a sealed stone his guess cracks open | A prediction raises curiosity and memory for the answer (Brod 2019; Kang 2009) |
| 6 | Mined-out sources | A source's few gems all solid marks it mined out | Sets get completed (Barasz 2017). Rewards finishing, so it needs his call against D62 |
| 7 | The assay | A level counts once he explains the skill's goal from memory | Achievements work when few and hard (Groening 2019) |
| 8 | Then and now | His first answer beside today's when a gem goes solid | Seeing their own results made learners value retrieval (Hui 2021) |
| 9 | Today's find | The end screen names the session's best recall | Peak-end (D177) |
| 10 | The deep cut | One optional harder exercise at the end | Effort evidence as 3, plus choice |

## Habit findings (nothing built)

- Automaticity took a median of 66 days, one missed day cost nothing, a missed week did (Lally 2009; Singh 2024). Routine and time cues worked equally (Keller 2021); reminders alone get repeats but not automaticity, and praise messages did nothing (Stawarz 2015). So the reminder plus the "After X, I review" line (D67) is the right pair, and the reminder needs setting on his Mac.
- Ideas not built: name the comeback in words ("Missed Oct 3. Got it today."), a backup line in the plan (g = 0.34, Wang 2021), a short return after days away, sound on three moments, the skill goal on the end screen.

## What to avoid

- A streak counter. Streaks do raise use (Cristia 2024, 60,000 pupils) and a highlighted broken one lowers it (Silverman & Barasch 2022); the counter becomes the goal (Moldon 2020). D23 is a choice, not a free win.
- Praise messages, points or anything spendable, rewards keyed to the backlog or to volume, a new number during an exercise, and social features for now (competence was the strongest need, Cerasoli 2016).
