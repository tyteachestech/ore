# Need Support and Need Thwarting: A Meta-Analysis of Autonomy, Competence, and Relatedness Supportive and Thwarting Behaviors in Student Populations

## Study Overview

- **Citation**: Howard, J. L., Slemp, G. R., & Wang, Y. (2024)
- **Study Type**: Meta-analysis
- **Sample**: k=542 studies, 637 samples, N=388,912 participants; mean age 16.3 years; 53.26% female; 42.5% classroom contexts, 33.5% educational experiences outside classrooms (with parents), 24% physical education
- **Scope**: 8,693 correlations synthesized; 72 covariates examined; 183 meta-analytic effects reported
- **Countries**: 56 national contexts; US (19.3%), China (9.8%), Spain (8.6%), Belgium (8.3%), Canada (7.5%)
- **DOI**: 10.1177/01461672231225364
- **Note**: Study design is correlational throughout — causal direction cannot be inferred. One highly influential outlier study (Wang et al., 2021, n=513,295) was excluded from analyses due to disproportionate weighting.

> **K-12 context flag:** The majority of samples are drawn from school-age student populations in classroom, parent, and physical education contexts. Mean age is 16.3 years. Transfer to adult async video learning is theoretically plausible (SDT is domain-general) but not directly established by this data.

---

## Key Findings

- **Finding 1 — Autonomy support broadly beneficial**: Autonomy support showed consistent positive correlations across learning outcomes, motivation, engagement, and well-being. Performance (general): r=.27 (95% CI [.24, .30], k=90, n=95,628). GPA: r=.11 (95% CI [.07, .14], k=64, n=49,621). Cognitive skills: r=.28 (95% CI [.21, .35], k=22). Emotional engagement: r=.44 (95% CI [.40, .49], k=61). Self-efficacy: r=.41 (95% CI [.36, .45], k=42). Valuation of education: r=.43 (95% CI [.38, .49], k=28).

- **Finding 2 — Thwarting is not just the absence of support**: Autonomy thwarting correlated negatively with performance (general): r=-.19 (95% CI [-.28, -.11], k=6) and GPA: r=-.26 (95% CI [-.44, -.08], k=9). Relative weights analysis (RWA) showed autonomy thwarting accounted for 78% of explained variance in GPA, while autonomy support accounted for only 14%. Thwarting was also the primary predictor of emotional ill-being (55% of variance) and externalized misconduct (53% of variance).

- **Finding 3 — Warmth is the key behavior**: The single strongest specific behavior correlating with both autonomy support (r=.57, k=14) and competence support (r=.61, k=7) was warmth — a relatedness-type behavior. Participatory/rationale behaviors correlated r=.53 (k=4) with autonomy support. Involvement correlated r=.44 (k=42) with autonomy support.

- **Finding 4 — Competence support does not predict academic performance**: Despite adequate sample sizes, competence support was unrelated to GPA (r=.03, k=12) and general academic performance (r=.05, k=16). Competence support was more strongly associated with self-regulation (RWA: 52% of variance) and behavioral engagement (RWA: 60% of variance).

- **Finding 5 — Relatedness support has unique value for self-efficacy and well-being**: Relatedness support showed r=.59 with self-efficacy (k=6) — the strongest correlate across all support types for that outcome. Relatedness support also correlated r=.46 with emotional well-being (k=5) and r=-.26 with depression (k=7) and anxiety (k=6). RWA indicated relatedness support uniquely predicted self-efficacy (60% of variance).

- **Finding 6 — Autonomy support promotes deep learning and metacognition**: Autonomy support correlated with deep learning strategies (r=.33, k=16), metacognition (r=.40, k=10), time management (r=.35, k=10), and goal setting (r=.34, k=10). Autonomy thwarting negatively correlated with metacognition (r=-.42, k=5).

- **Finding 7 — Three support types are highly intercorrelated**: Autonomy, competence, and relatedness support intercorrelated strongly (r=.64–.68). The three thwarting types also intercorrelated strongly (r=.56–.66). This raises incremental validity questions — each type may not add unique predictive value beyond a general "support" factor in many outcome domains.

- **Finding 8 — Supporter well-being matters**: Parents with higher need satisfaction were perceived as more autonomy supportive (r=.37, k=14). Parent need frustration (r=-.19, k=8) and ill-being (r=-.14, k=8) negatively correlated with autonomy support provision. Teacher well-being trend was positive but non-significant (r=.15, k=4).

## Mechanisms & Moderators

- **SDT causal sequence**: Support/thwarting behaviors → need satisfaction/frustration → motivation types → distal outcomes (performance, well-being). This meta-analysis tests only the antecedent-to-behavior and behavior-to-outcome links; the need satisfaction mediating step is reported in supplemental materials.

- **Dual-path model**: Supportive behaviors associate primarily with positive outcomes; thwarting behaviors associate primarily with negative outcomes. RWA confirms these are not perfectly mirrored: thwarting uniquely predicts GPA decline (78% of variance), emotional ill-being, and misconduct more strongly than the positive paths of support.

- **Warmth as cross-cutting mechanism**: Warmth (a relatedness-type behavior) was the strongest specific behavior correlate for both autonomy and competence support composites (r=.57 and .61). This suggests relational quality underlies perceived support across all three need categories.

- **Chaos (competence thwarting) is orthogonal to autonomy support**: Chaos correlated r=-.07 with autonomy support (non-significant) but r=-.20 with competence support — suggesting chaotic/unpredictable environments specifically undermine competence perceptions, not autonomy.

- **Student personality as moderator of perceived support**: Agreeableness (r=.23), conscientiousness (r=.27), extraversion (r=.17), and openness (r=.15) all positively predicted perceived autonomy support; neuroticism negatively predicted it (r=-.17). Students' autonomous causality orientation also predicted perceived autonomy support (r=.28, k=12).

- **Supporter well-being as moderator**: Parents with greater need satisfaction provided more autonomy support (r=.37). Parental need frustration and ill-being were negative predictors (r=-.19, -.14). Implication: supporter psychological health is an upstream condition for need-supportive behavior provision.

- **Age moderator**: Student age negatively correlated with perceived autonomy support (r=-.04, k=65) — small but significant, suggesting older students perceive or receive less support. Competence and relatedness support were unrelated to age.

- **RWA differential contributions (Table 12)**:
  - GPA: Autonomy thwarting 78%, autonomy support 14%, relatedness support 6%
  - Self-regulation: Competence support 52%, autonomy support 15%
  - Self-efficacy: Relatedness support 60%, autonomy support 18%, competence support 18%
  - Engagement (behavioral): Autonomy support 60%
  - Engagement (emotional): Competence support 29%, autonomy support 26%
  - Emotional well-being (PA): Relatedness support 36%, competence support 20%
  - Emotional ill-being (NA): Autonomy thwarting 55%
  - Externalized misconduct: Autonomy thwarting 53%

## Study Design Details

- **Design**: Correlational meta-analysis (random effects model throughout). Not pre-registered.
- **Analysis tool**: Psychmeta package (R), composite variable function used to handle non-independent effects within studies.
- **Reliability correction**: Average alpha coefficients imputed for missing reliability data; attenuation corrections applied to effect size estimates.
- **Outlier handling**: Wang et al. (2021, n=513,295) excluded from all analyses due to disproportionate weighting; sensitivity analyses conducted.
- **Interrater reliability**: 35% of initial studies double-coded; 96.39% agreement across all coding decisions.
- **Publication bias**: Eggers test across 175 effects; 32 significant results (18.3%). Funnel plots available on OSF.
- **Covariates excluded**: Any covariate appearing in fewer than 5 studies was excluded as insufficient for meta-analysis.

## Limitations

- **Correlational only**: Direction of causality cannot be established. Students who perform well may elicit more supportive teacher behavior — simultaneity bias is explicitly acknowledged.
- **High heterogeneity throughout**: I² consistently above 85–99% for most outcomes. The pooled r values should be interpreted as rough central estimates with wide true-effect distributions.
- **Incremental validity concern**: Three support types intercorrelate r=.64–.68. RWA addresses multicollinearity partially, but whether each type adds genuine unique value beyond a general support factor remains unresolved.
- **Thwarting literature is thinner**: Sample sizes for thwarting analyses are considerably smaller than support analyses, limiting power for moderator detection.
- **Specific behavior taxonomy incomplete**: Cross-correlations between specific behaviors within broader support categories (e.g., warmth vs. involvement vs. structure) could not be estimated — a gap in the search strategy.
- **Mean age 16.3 years**: The vast majority of data is from adolescent school populations. Adult learner generalizability is untested within this dataset.
- **Physical education overrepresented**: 24% of samples come from PE contexts, which may inflate some engagement and motivation estimates for educational content delivery contexts.
- **No causal / experimental synthesis**: Intervention RCTs exist in the SDT literature (Su & Reeve, 2011; Ntoumanis et al., 2021) but are not the focus here. Effect sizes here are observational correlations, not intervention effects.
- **Potential confound — teaching quality and autonomy support**: The correlation between perceived teaching quality and autonomy support (r=.49) is identical in magnitude to the correlation between perceived teacher relationship quality and autonomy support — raising concern about measurement overlap.

## Population Scope Assessment

- **Population**: Student (primarily K-12 and physical education; some university; mean age 16.3)
- **Relevance to Tyler's async video context**: Medium. SDT is explicitly domain-general — the three basic needs apply to any learning context, including self-directed adult learners watching tutorials. However, the behavioral operationalizations here (teacher control, helicopter parenting, classroom structure) do not map directly onto content creator behaviors. The conceptual translation is strong; the empirical grounding for adult async video specifically is indirect.
- **Best-applicable concepts**: Autonomy support (giving rationale, offering choices, avoiding external pressure) and thwarting avoidance are most directly transferable. Warmth as a relational behavior maps to creator persona and parasocial connection.
- **Red flags for scope**: No adult learner data. No async or self-directed learning context. No creator-economy samples.

> **K-12 context flag:** This finding comes from classroom settings with younger learners. Transfer to adult async video is plausible but not established — treat with appropriate weight reduction.

## Conclusions

"Results indicate that teachers and parents who experience psychological need satisfaction and well-being are seen as more supportive. Supportive teacher behaviors correlated positively with a range of desired student outcomes, including performance, engagement, and well-being. Thwarting behaviors tended to display the opposite pattern." The meta-analysis provides the strongest available quantitative summary of SDT need support effects in education, but also raises important questions about the incremental validity of distinguishing three need types given their high intercorrelations. The finding that thwarting has asymmetrically stronger effects on negative outcomes (GPA decline, ill-being, misconduct) than support has on positive outcomes is a key theoretical advance. Warmth emerged as the single most potent specific behavior across both autonomy and competence support categories.

## Named Frameworks (if applicable)

- **Framework**: Self-Determination Theory (SDT) — Ryan & Deci (2017)
  - Components: Autonomy need, Competence need, Relatedness need; Need Support vs. Need Thwarting; Dual-path model
  - Research alignment:
    - Autonomy support -> performance (r=.27), engagement (r=.37–.44), well-being (r=.35–.38) — supported
    - Competence support -> self-regulation (RWA 52%), behavioral engagement (RWA 60%) — supported; NOT performance
    - Relatedness support -> self-efficacy (r=.59), emotional well-being (r=.46) — supported
    - Thwarting as distinct from support (not merely its inverse) -> supported by RWA; partially supported by asymmetric correlations
    - Need satisfaction as mediator -> not tested in main analyses; supplemental only

## Extraction Notes

This is a high-quality, large-scale meta-analysis (N=388,912) and is arguably the most comprehensive quantitative synthesis of SDT need support in educational contexts to date. Several points are particularly important for the Teaching Guidebook:

1. **For Tyler specifically**: The warmth finding is directly actionable. Warmth (genuine interest, care, relational attunement) is the strongest predictor of perceived support — more than explicit autonomy-granting behaviors or structure. This maps directly to creator presence and parasocial warmth in video content.

2. **Autonomy thwarting asymmetry**: The finding that thwarting is a stronger predictor of negative outcomes than support is of positive outcomes is practically significant. Avoiding controlling, pressure-laden, or dismissive framing in tutorials may matter more than actively providing autonomy support.

3. **Competence support ≠ performance gains**: Structure and feedback (competence support) predicted self-regulation and engagement but NOT GPA or academic performance. This is counterintuitive and worth flagging — didactic clarity alone does not predict learning outcomes; motivational climate matters more.

4. **High I² throughout**: Effect sizes should be treated as rough central estimates. The wide credibility intervals (80% CIs) indicate genuine heterogeneity — effects vary considerably across populations, contexts, and measurement approaches.

5. **Adult context caveat**: This paper's population is predominantly adolescent students. The SDT framework transfers, but the specific correlational estimates may not hold at the same magnitude for self-directed adult learners choosing to watch tutorials voluntarily (where baseline motivation is presumably higher and autonomy thwarting harder to operationalize).
