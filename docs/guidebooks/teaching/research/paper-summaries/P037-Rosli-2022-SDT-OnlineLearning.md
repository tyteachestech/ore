# Self-Determination Theory and Online Learning in University: Advancements, Future Direction and Research Gaps

## Study Overview
- **Citation**: Rosli, M.S., Saleh, N.S., Ali, A.M., & Abu Bakar, S. (2022). *Sustainability*, 14(21), 14655.
- **Study Type**: Systematic literature review (PRISMA)
- **Sample**: k=49 articles drawn from Scopus and Web of Science (initial pool: 178, after deduplication: 121, after eligibility screening: 49)
- **Search date**: September 2022; no publication year restriction
- **DOI**: 10.3390/su142114655
- **PMID**: unknown

> **MOOC/Higher-Ed context flag:** All 49 reviewed studies were conducted in university settings. Closest to Tyler's context among education research, but note institutional framing, mandatory enrollment, and graded stakes differ from voluntary async video learning.

---

## Key Findings

- **Finding 1 — SDT factor dominance**: Autonomy, competence, and relatedness (ACR) were the most prevalent SDT elements, appearing in 35 of 49 articles (71.43%). Only 26.53% (n=13) of articles did not include ACR as core components. The basic psychological needs triad is the dominant operational lens for online learning motivation research.

- **Finding 2 — Full-spectrum SDT is underused**: Only 9 articles (18.37%) used the "full spectrum" of SDT (autonomy, competence, relatedness + intrinsic motivation, identified regulation, external regulation, amotivation). 63% of articles used ACR alone without extended SDT factors. This is identified as a research gap — the full continuum is rarely studied as a single integrated framework.

- **Finding 3 — SDT integration with other theories is rare**: Only 9 of 49 articles (18.37%) incorporated other theories alongside SDT. Theories integrated include ARCS, McClelland's needs, Engagement Theory, Control-Value Theory, Social Cognitive Theory, Constructivism, Task-Technology Fit (TTF), Social Motivation Theory, Cognitive Coping Theory, and UTAUT. The dominant trend is SDT studied in isolation.

- **Finding 4 — Student sample monopoly**: 85.9% of sampled article participants were students. Lecturers/instructors were sampled in only 3 articles (6.1%). Mixed samples (lecturers + students) appeared in 4 articles (8.16%). The instructor's motivational experience of online learning is a near-complete research gap.

- **Finding 5 — Quantitative methods dominate**: 29 of 49 articles (59.18%) used quantitative methods. Mixed methods accounted for 22.45%. Qualitative methods appeared in only ~18%. Structural Equation Modeling (CB-SEM or PLS-SEM) was the dominant analysis technique. Qualitative and mixed-methods approaches are identified as underdeveloped future directions.

- **Finding 6 — Technology acceptance models are emerging integration partners**: A secondary trend is integrating SDT with acceptance models (TAM, UTAUT). This is inferred to result from the additive structure of acceptance models, which invite external variable addition. Future SDT research is anticipated to combine motivational needs with technology acceptance frameworks.

- **Finding 7 — Amotivation and intrinsic motivation are under-studied SDT factors**: Among articles using the full or extended spectrum, intrinsic motivation, identified regulation, external regulation, and amotivation appeared together in 9 articles (18.37%). Amotivation is identified by name as a neglected factor worth incorporating — absence of motivation is as theoretically relevant as its presence.

## Effect Sizes (Raw Values)

This is a systematic review of trends and research gaps, not a meta-analysis. No pooled effect sizes are reported. The paper synthesizes article-level patterns (frequencies, percentages) rather than extracting statistical outcomes from primary studies.

- No d, SMD, OR, or r values reported.
- No heterogeneity statistics applicable.
- Quantitative patterns are descriptive (article counts and percentages across 49 studies).

## Mechanisms & Moderators

**Mechanisms described (from reviewed literature, not primary data):**

- **ACR as technology acceptance predictors**: Autonomy, competence, and relatedness together function as a single construct predicting technology-enhanced learning acceptance (citing Rosli & Saleh, 2022, ref [3]). When separated, each independently shapes motivation quality.
- **Intrinsic motivation → engagement**: Intrinsic motivation, originating from Cognitive Evaluation Theory (CET), is positioned as a core motivational nutrient. It is rarely studied without adjacent SDT factors.
- **Amotivation → disengagement**: Amotivation (absence of self-determination) significantly influences students' technology acceptance. Described as part of the "full spectrum" that most studies neglect.
- **External regulation → internalization pathway**: Extrinsic motivation includes a continuum from external regulation (least autonomous) through introjection, identification, to integration (most autonomous). This continuum is rarely modeled in full in online learning research.

**Moderators:**

- **Geography**: US and Asian universities dominate (25% North America, 39% Asia). Africa and South America are near-absent — limits generalizability of findings.
- **Sample type**: Student-only vs. mixed samples may produce different motivational profiles. The less unidimensional perspective of mixed samples is noted as unstudied.
- **Synchronous vs. asynchronous delivery**: Not explicitly analyzed as a moderator in this review, but presence of studies from both contexts implies variance. Async contexts (closest to Tyler's) are not disaggregated.
- **COVID-19 context**: Several reviewed studies conducted during pandemic-forced online learning — motivation dynamics under coercion may differ from voluntary online participation.

## Study Design Details

- **Review type**: PRISMA-compliant systematic literature review
- **Search databases**: Scopus and Web of Science
- **Inclusion criteria**: Empirical research on SDT in online learning at universities, written in English, with clear SDT framework element
- **Exclusion criteria**: Non-university populations (K-12, etc.), reviews (not empirical), non-English, absent SDT framework
- **Deduplication**: 57 duplicates removed from initial 178; 28 not retrievable; 44 excluded by eligibility criteria → 49 final articles
- **Analysis method**: Descriptive synthesis across 6 dimensions: theory integration, SDT factors used, sample type, research approach, geographic location, future work recommendations
- **No quality scoring or risk-of-bias assessment** was conducted on included studies — the review characterizes the field's patterns rather than evaluating individual study quality

## Limitations

- **No effect size extraction**: The review describes what SDT factors were studied and how, not what they found. No synthesis of motivational outcomes, learning gains, or engagement effects.
- **No quality appraisal**: No risk-of-bias rating or quality rubric applied to the 49 included studies. A low-quality quantitative study carries the same weight as a high-quality RCT in the frequency counts.
- **Search cutoff artifacts**: Data search conducted September 2022 — some late 2022 articles may be missing. No pre-specified year cutoff used, which is comprehensive but makes forward extension ambiguous.
- **Two databases only**: Scopus and Web of Science. Studies indexed elsewhere (e.g., ERIC for education research) may be absent.
- **University-only scope**: Explicitly excludes K-12 and non-university contexts — relevant adult learning outside universities (MOOCs, corporate training, creator platforms) is not represented.
- **Aggregation obscures outcomes**: Knowing that 71% of studies used ACR says nothing about whether autonomy, competence, or relatedness actually predicted learning outcomes — this is a map of the research field, not a map of the phenomena.
- **No distinction between async and sync delivery**: This is a material limitation for Tyler's use case. Voluntary async video viewing is motivationally distinct from synchronous online courses, but the review does not disaggregate this.

## Population Scope Assessment

- **Population**: University students (85.9% of reviewed study samples); some lecturer samples (6.1%)
- **Relevance to async video tutorial creator (Tyler's context)**: Medium — university online learning is closer to Tyler's context than K-12 or clinical, but still differs in key ways:
  - Learners in reviewed studies are enrolled, often graded, sometimes compelled by institutional context
  - Tyler's viewers are voluntary, episodic, self-directed — relatedness to a creator (parasocial) rather than institutional
  - Competence needs in tutorial-watching are self-defined (curiosity-driven) vs. curriculum-defined
- **Red flags**: None for clinical/addiction scope. Standard MOOC/higher-ed caution applies.
- **Transferable insight**: The ACR framework and the full SDT spectrum concepts are theoretically applicable to tutorial design — especially autonomy (viewer agency over pace, depth) and competence (appropriate challenge calibration). The review itself does not provide actionable effect sizes, but points toward which theoretical constructs are most studied and which gaps exist.

---

## Conclusions

"SDT is a central motivational theory that has received considerable attention in education. As the significance of online learning increased, the knowledge gap regarding advancements, future directions, and research gaps in this field of study became more important." The review concludes that ACR dominates current research but the full spectrum of SDT (including amotivation, intrinsic motivation, identified and external regulation) remains underutilized in a single integrated study. Future research should integrate SDT with acceptance and cognitive theories, expand samples to include instructors, and use larger longitudinal designs.

## Named Frameworks

- **Self-Determination Theory (SDT)** — Deci, Connell, & Ryan (1989)
  - Components: Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness (basic psychological needs); Intrinsic Motivation; Extrinsic Motivation continuum (External Regulation → Introjection → Identification → Integration); Amotivation
  - Research alignment from reviewed literature:
    - Autonomy → perceived learner control, self-pacing, choice in online environments
    - Competence → perceived skill-building, mastery experiences in online tasks
    - Relatedness → social presence, peer interaction, instructor connection in online settings
    - Amotivation → dropout risk, disengagement, non-adoption of online tools
    - Full spectrum → rarely integrated; identified as primary research gap

- **Five Mini-Theories of SDT** (Vansteenkiste et al., 2020, ref [77])
  - Cognitive Evaluation Theory (CET)
  - Organismic Integration Theory
  - Causality Orientations Theory
  - Basic Psychological Need Theory
  - Goal Content Theory
  - Note: Most reviewed studies invoke only Basic Psychological Need Theory (ACR); CET (intrinsic motivation) appears secondarily

## Extraction Notes

This paper is a **field map**, not an evidence base. Its value for the Teaching Guidebook is:

1. **Theoretical vocabulary**: Confirms ACR as the operative SDT shorthand used across online learning research. When Tyler reads other SDT papers, ACR is the shared language.

2. **Gap identification**: The finding that 81.6% of studies use SDT in isolation (no theory integration) and 63% use only ACR (no extended motivation spectrum) signals that research supporting full-SDT-based tutorial design is sparse. Claims derived from SDT about tutorial design should note this limitation.

3. **Async delivery gap**: The review does not disaggregate synchronous from asynchronous delivery, and neither context maps cleanly to voluntary YouTube consumption. This is an unstated but important limitation for this guidebook's application.

4. **No effect sizes to extract**: Do not use this paper as a source for quantitative claims. Use it for theoretical framing, as a pointer toward the ACR/SDT literature, and to note what the field has not yet studied (instructor motivation, full-spectrum SDT, async contexts).

5. **Priority score of 6.5 is appropriate**: Domain relevance (learner motivation) is high, but evidence grade is limited by the review's descriptive-only design. Weight accordingly — useful for framing, not for quantitative claims.
