# The Instructor Presence Effect and Its Moderators in Instructional Video: A Series of Meta-Analyses

## Study Overview

- **Citation**: Beege, M., Schroeder, N. L., Heidig, S., Rey, G. D., & Schneider, S. (2023)
- **Study Type**: Series of 7 meta-analyses (separate meta-analysis per outcome variable), random-effects throughout
- **Sample**: N=6339 total (N=3367 visible instructor condition); 35 studies, 46 pair-wise comparisons
- **Mean age**: 23.23 years; 66.7% female
- **Search period**: 1990–September 15, 2022
- **Databases**: ERIC, SSCI, PsycINFO, PSYNDEX, Google Scholar; unpublished work solicited
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.edurev.2023.100564
- **Journal**: Educational Research Review, 41 (2023) 100564
- **Open access**: CC BY license

## Key Findings

- **Retention (small positive, significant)**: Visible instructor produces a small but statistically significant improvement in retention (ability to recall/recognize factual information). g+=0.22, 95% CI [0.07, 0.37], SE=0.08, z=2.89, p=.004. Very high heterogeneity: I²=90.76%, Q=455.75, df=38, p<.001.

- **Transfer (null, non-significant)**: No significant effect on transfer performance (ability to apply knowledge to new contexts). g+=0.06, 95% CI [-0.09, 0.21], SE=0.08, z=0.74, p=.46. Still highly heterogeneous: I²=75.61%, Q=89.18, df=19, p<.001.

- **Social presence (medium positive, significant)**: Visible instructor significantly enhanced perceived social presence (sense of interacting with a social entity). g+=0.35, 95% CI [0.08, 0.62], SE=0.14, z=2.58, p=.01. Very high heterogeneity: I²=95.44%, Q=399.95, df=21, p<.001.

- **Extraneous cognitive load (reduced -- beneficial direction)**: Contrary to split-attention predictions, instructor presence REDUCED perceived extraneous cognitive load. g+=0.31, 95% CI [0.11, 0.51], SE=0.10, z=2.98, p=.003. Heterogeneous: I²=86.24%, Q=79.39, df=13, p<.001. Note: positive g+ = ECL reduced = beneficial.

- **Dwell time on instructional material (large reduction -- attention diverted)**: Instructor presence significantly reduced the time learners spent fixating on learning content. g+=-1.81, 95% CI [-2.49, -1.12], SE=0.35, z=-5.18, p<.001. Heterogeneous: I²=77.58%, Q=35.69, df=7, p<.001. Learners looked at the instructor instead of the slide content -- split attention effect confirmed via eye-tracking, yet learning (retention) still improved.

- **Affect (medium positive, significant)**: Visible instructor increased affective/emotional ratings. g+=0.43, 95% CI [0.18, 0.67], SE=0.13, z=3.37, p<.001. Very high heterogeneity: I²=97.81%, Q=850.14, df=21, p<.001.

- **Motivation (medium positive, significant)**: Visible instructor increased motivational ratings. g+=0.41, 95% CI [0.14, 0.68], SE=0.14, z=2.96, p=.003. Very high heterogeneity: I²=93.25%, Q=128.55, df=13, p<.001.

## Mechanisms & Moderators

**Theoretical framework**: CASTLE (Cognitive-Affective-Social Theory of Learning in digital Environments; Schneider et al., 2021), extending CTML and CATLM.

- **Cognitive Influence Hypothesis**: Social cues influence selection and organization of information (cognitive pathway)
- **Interaction Hypothesis**: Social cues influence motivational and emotional processes (affective pathway)

**Results re CASTLE:**
- Interaction Hypothesis FULLY SUPPORTED: affect (+), motivation (+), social presence (+) all significant
- Cognitive Influence Hypothesis PARTIALLY SUPPORTED: ECL reduced (positive), retention improved; BUT dwell time on instructional material reduced (attentional distraction confirmed) AND transfer null. The cognitive pathway appears to operate: social processing reduces perceived ECL, but attentional diversion to the instructor reduces time-on-task. Net effect on retention: small positive. Net effect on transfer: zero.

## Study Design Details

- **Inclusion**: Real human instructors filmed for video; visible vs. not-visible control; dynamic video (not static images); head minimally visible and always dynamic
- **Exclusion**: Modeling/example videos (separate construct), pedagogical agent studies (non-human), studies without a no-instructor control, static instructor images, studies without enough statistical data for effect size calculation
- **k breakdown**: 35 studies total; k=30 included in moderator analysis (5 excluded for no learning outcome measures); k=46 pair-wise comparisons
- **Setting**: Laboratory k=19, field study k=5, online study k=17
- **Language**: English k=16, Chinese k=8, German k=15, other k=7
- **Pacing**: System-paced k=33, learner-paced k=8
- **Average video duration**: ~13 min (M=788.26 s, SD=598.96 s)
- **Instructor integration**: Greenscreen k=16, camera (with visible background) k=21
- **Image detail**: Head only k=18, upper body k=17 (1 study showed whole body)
- **Mean instructor frame size**: 14.82% of video (Min=3%, Max=50%)
- **Instructor position**: Fixed k=33, variable k=3; right side k=29, left k=5, middle k=2
- **Effect size metric**: Hedges g+ (small-sample-adjusted); random-effects model (Hedges & Vevea, 1998)
- **Software**: JASP v0.16.3, R with metafor package, MetaREG (SPSS) for continuous moderators
- **Inter-rater reliability**: Cohen's κ=0.87–1.0 for all coded study features
- **Outlier detection**: Grubbs (1969) + Viechtbauer & Cheung (2010)

## Limitations

- **Very high heterogeneity in all outcomes**: I² ranges from 75-98%. The overall g+ values represent means across highly variable studies. Individual study effects range from strongly negative (g=-0.8) to strongly positive (g=1.2). Overall estimates must be interpreted cautiously.

- **Most moderator analyses underpowered**: The majority of moderator subgroup cells have k=1-5 comparisons. Authors explicitly call these "descriptive trends" and warn against strong conclusions. The moderator finding for instructor size (β=-0.51) is based on a continuous regression and is the most statistically robust moderator finding.

- **Dominant population is university students in lab settings**: k=30 of 46 comparisons from university students; k=19 from laboratory experiments. Laboratory setting showed significantly larger effects than online studies for retention (g+=0.37 lab vs. 0.06 online). Real-world (YouTube) effects are likely smaller than the overall g+=0.22.

- **Post-hoc moderator coding**: Moderators coded from published studies, not pre-registered. Confounding between moderators (e.g., historical science studies may have also used larger instructor images) cannot be ruled out.

- **Broad outcome categorization**: Affect, satisfaction, and positive activation grouped together. Transfer "near" vs. "far" not distinguished. May mask important within-category differences.

- **No mediation analysis**: CASTLE's mediation hypothesis (social processes mediate cognitive processing) not testable with available aggregated data.

- **Learner pacing underrepresented**: Only k=8 learner-paced comparisons. Findings primarily apply to system-paced video (like standard YouTube where viewers can pause/replay but the video isn't interactive).

- **No creator-economy studies**: All studies from academic/educational contexts. Learner motivation, attention, and engagement patterns differ from voluntary YouTube viewing.

- **No conflict of interest** declared by any author.

## Population Scope Assessment

- **Population**: Predominantly university students (k=30/46 comparisons); adult education k=7; secondary students k=1; no primary school data
- **Setting**: Laboratory experiments (k=19) dominate; online studies (k=17) closer to Tyler's context
- **Relevance to Tyler's async YouTube context**: Medium. The online study subset is most applicable. The informatics/software domain finding (g+=0.70) directly matches Tyler's Mac/AI content domain. However: all studies involve learners with some educational obligation (course videos, assigned materials) -- not self-selected YouTube viewing.
- **Context flag**: MOOC/higher-ed context applies. Note that the online study sub-effect for retention (g+=0.06, ns) is much smaller than the laboratory effect (g+=0.37). This is the most relevant estimate for Tyler's context.

> **MOOC context flag:** The majority of included studies come from controlled educational settings -- university lecture videos and laboratory experiments. The online study subset (k=17) is more relevant to Tyler's context and shows a much smaller, non-significant retention effect (g+=0.06 vs. g+=0.37 for lab). Weight accordingly when applying to YouTube tutorial design.

- **Red flags**: None. Non-clinical, non-addiction population. Broad multimedia learning domain.

## Conclusions

"The meta-analyses presented here outline positive effects concerning multiple learning-relevant processes on retention performance. Exploratory moderator analyses gave insights into the boundary conditions of the effects of instructor presence but future research should focus on conducting appropriate experimental research to further specify the instructor presence effect."

**Authors' practical recommendations** (Section 5.1): Including a visible instructor is associated with motivational, affective, and social benefits as well as enhanced retention. No positive transfer effect was found. Practical guidance: (1) Do not make the instructor image salient/large -- a corner placement is recommended. (2) Larger instructor images may have no beneficial effects and might further distract learners. (3) If visual material is complex (nuanced diagrams), consider removing the instructor's image to free attentional resources for content. (4) If the supplementary visual material is primarily narration-support, the instructor's image seems to help perceptive outcomes and retention.

**On the ECL paradox**: "A visible instructor seemed not to overload the visual channel per se. One possible explanation might lie directly in the split-attention effect...averting the gaze from the material and looking at the instructor can lower the subjective perception of visual load, especially considering that including social and non-verbal information could be categorized as a fundamental biological process that does not induce heavy cognitive load."

## Named Frameworks

- **CASTLE** (Cognitive-Affective-Social Theory of Learning in digital Environments; Schneider et al., 2021)
  - Cognitive Influence Hypothesis: social cues affect selection and organization of information
  - Interaction Hypothesis: social cues affect motivational and emotional processes
  - This paper's findings: Interaction Hypothesis fully supported; Cognitive Influence partially supported

- **CTML** (Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning; Mayer, 2005): CASTLE extends this. The standard CTML distraction prediction (visible face → increased extraneous load → worse learning) is NOT supported by these meta-analytic results. ECL was reduced, not increased.

## Conflict Documentation: P003 vs. P023 Alemdağ vs. P049 Gu

### Updated conflict status post-PDF extraction

**P003 Beege (2023) -- confirmed from PDF:**
- Retention: g+=0.22 (significant, small)
- Transfer: g+=0.06 (null)
- ECL: g+=0.31 (significantly REDUCED)
- Dwell time: g+=-1.81 (significantly reduced -- attentional distraction confirmed)
- Instructor size is the primary transfer moderator (β=-0.51)
- Online study subset: retention g+=0.06, ns (much weaker than overall)

**P023 Alemdağ (2022) -- k=20 studies (prior LLM-sourced extraction):**
- Instructor presence effects on learning and social presence: NOT statistically significant
- Cognitive load: INCREASED (opposite direction to Beege)
- Motivation: increased
- Citation health: 8 supporting / 5 contrasting -- actively contested

**Conflict resolution with Alemdağ**: Beege is the more authoritative paper (k=35 vs. k=20, more recent literature included, broader outcomes tested). The ECL direction conflict may be explained by Beege's finding that larger instructor images harm transfer -- Alemdağ's included studies may have used larger images on average, driving the ECL increase finding. Beege's significant reduction in ECL is likely driven by studies where the instructor image was small (corner PIP-style) and the dominant cognitive experience was reduced attentional fragmentation via social engagement.

**P049 Gu et al. (2024) -- neuroimaging + eye-tracking, N=73:**
- Transfer: SIGNIFICANTLY POSITIVE (directly contradicts Beege's null transfer finding)
- Dwell time on slides: reduced (consistent with Beege's eye-tracking finding)
- Neural synchronization enhanced in socio-emotional and working memory regions
- Mechanism proposed: benefit-cost trade-off where socio-emotional processing outweighs attentional distraction cost

**Conflict resolution with Gu**: Gu's transfer finding (single experiment, n=57) conflicts with Beege's null transfer (k=20 comparisons). Meta-analytic evidence is structurally stronger. However, Gu's neuroimaging provides mechanism data unavailable to meta-analysis. The instructor size moderator may explain the conflict: if Gu's experimental videos used small, well-placed instructor images, the conditions were favorable for transfer (consistent with Beege's β=-0.51 moderator). The two findings can be reconciled: instructor presence CAN improve transfer under optimal conditions (small image, high-quality implementation), but on AVERAGE across studies it does not.

**Synthesis for chapter writing:**
1. Instructor presence reliably improves retention (small effect), social presence, affect, and motivation
2. Transfer effect: null on average but potentially positive with optimal implementation (small instructor image, no prior knowledge learners)
3. The distraction mechanism IS real (dwell time reduced, learners look at instructor not content) but does not eliminate the retention benefit -- likely because affective/motivational gains compensate
4. Larger instructor image = worse transfer (only robust moderator for transfer, β=-0.51)
5. The practical implication for Tyler: a camera feed in the corner of the frame (head visible, small proportion of screen) is the evidence-supported format

## Extraction Notes

**Quality assessment**: This is the most comprehensive meta-analysis on instructor presence as of 2023. PRISMA guidelines followed. Random-effects model throughout (appropriate given high heterogeneity). Publication bias assessed with two methods. Inter-rater reliability high (κ=0.87-1.0). The high heterogeneity (I²=75-98%) is a real limitation -- it indicates that the average effect size obscures massive study-level variation. The specific moderator findings are often more useful than the overall g+ values.

**Critical observation on the online study subset**: The overall retention effect (g+=0.22) is dominated by laboratory studies (g+=0.37). The online study subset effect (g+=0.06, ns) is the estimate most relevant to Tyler's YouTube context and is NOT significant. This distinction was not available in the prior LLM extraction and substantially changes the strength of the recommendation. For Tyler's context: the motivational/affective benefits (g+=0.41-0.43) are likely the primary real-world benefit of camera presence, not the learning outcomes.

**Tyler-specific relevance**: The informatics/software domain finding (g+=0.70 for retention, k=6) is the strongest single domain-specific finding and directly matches Tyler's Mac/AI tutorial content. Camera integration (vs. greenscreen) showed significant retention effects (g+=0.24). Head-only image detail showed significant retention effects (g+=0.25). These converge on a specific recommendation: webcam feed (not greenscreen composite), head visible, small frame proportion, placed in corner -- consistent with standard YouTube tutorial PIP format.
