# A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Self-Determination-Theory-Based Interventions in the Education Context

## Study Overview
- **Citation**: Wang, Y., Wang, H., Wang, S., Wind, S. A., & Gill, C. (2024)
- **Study Type**: Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA)
- **Sample**: Systematic review: N=11,792 participants across 36 studies; Meta-analysis: N=9,433 participants across 31 studies (137 effect sizes)
- **Databases searched**: PsycINFO, ERIC, Web of Science, Google Scholar, Scopus, Medline, ScienceDirect, PSYNDEX, ProQuest Dissertations; search through June 2022
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.lmot.2024.102015
- **PMID**: unknown

> **K-12 context flag:** The majority of the 36 included studies were conducted in K-12 school settings with students ages 7-18. Seven studies involved university/adult samples. Transfer to Tyler's adult async video context is plausible for the underlying motivational mechanisms (autonomy, competence, relatedness), but the intervention designs (teacher training, classroom SDT programs) are not directly applicable. Weight the directional findings on need satisfaction; discount the magnitudes.

---

## Key Findings

- **Finding 1 — Autonomy support (group differences)**: SDT interventions produced a large effect on students' perceived autonomy support relative to control groups (g=1.14, 95% CI [0.90, 1.77], p<0.0001, k=22). Heterogeneity was extreme (I^2=99.42%, tau^2=6.09). Sensitivity analysis: removing Montero-Carretero et al. (2021) changed effect from g=1.65 to g=1.14. No publication bias detected (Egger's z=-0.33, p=0.66).

- **Finding 2 — Autonomy support (pre-post differences)**: Pre-post differences within intervention groups on autonomy support were significant (g=0.63, 95% CI [0.06, 1.21], p<0.01, k=14). Substantial heterogeneity (I^2=97.95%, tau^2=0.72). Excluding Zheng et al. (2020) changed effect from g=0.63 to g=0.19. No publication bias (Egger's z=3.65, p=0.10).

- **Finding 3 — Intrinsic motivation (group differences)**: SDT interventions produced a partially significant group effect on intrinsic motivation (g=0.58, 95% CI [0.26, 0.89], p<0.01, k=26). This is the bias-corrected figure after trim-and-fill (initial pooled: g=0.96; trim-and-fill corrected: g=0.58). Heterogeneity was extreme (I^2=98.46%, tau^2=1.35). Wide prediction interval (-1.57, 3.08) means effects may be near zero or negative in some contexts. Nine outliers identified; sensitivity analysis without outliers: g=0.47 (95% CI [0.28, 0.67]).

- **Finding 4 — Intrinsic motivation (pre-post differences)**: Pre-post differences within intervention groups on intrinsic motivation were not significant (g=0.12, 95% CI [0.17, 0.41], p>0.05, k=23). After trim-and-fill: g=0.02 (95% CI [-0.12, 0.15]), p>0.05, k=21. The intervention shows group advantage but not reliable within-group growth over time.

- **Finding 5 — Competence (group differences)**: No significant group difference in competence between intervention and control groups (g=0.80, 95% CI [-0.49, 1.65], p>0.05, k=17). After trim-and-fill: g=0.48 (95% CI [0.17, 0.79], p<0.01, k=16). Extreme heterogeneity (I^2=98.58%).

- **Finding 6 — Competence (pre-post differences)**: Pre-post differences within intervention groups on competence were significant (g=0.58, 95% CIs [0.01, 1.15], p<0.05, k=9). After removing one outlier (Zheng et al., 2020): g=0.21 (95% CI [0.1, 0.32], p<0.05, k=8). Substantial heterogeneity (I^2=94.78%).

- **Finding 7 — Relatedness (non-significant)**: Relatedness effects were non-significant for both group differences (g=1.66, 95% CI [-0.51, 3.83], p>0.05, k=12) and pre-post differences (g=1.43, 95% CI [-0.23, 3.09], p>0.05, k=10). Extreme heterogeneity in both (I^2=99.64% and 99.65% respectively). Removing Assor et al. (2018) as outlier changed group effect from g=1.66 to g=0.44, remaining non-significant.

- **Finding 8 — Net summary**: SDT interventions successfully support autonomy and competence in educational settings, produce a partially significant effect on intrinsic motivation, but do not reliably satisfy relatedness needs.

## Effect Sizes (Raw Values)

**Autonomy support:**
- Group difference: g=1.65 (initial), g=1.14 (bias-corrected, 95% CI [0.90, 1.77]), k=22, I^2=99.42%, tau^2=6.09
- Pre-post difference: g=0.63 (initial, 95% CI [0.06, 1.21], k=14), g=0.19 after outlier removal (95% CI [0.04, 0.95])

**Intrinsic motivation:**
- Group difference: g=0.96 (initial), g=0.58 (bias-corrected, 95% CI [0.26, 0.89], p<0.01, k=26), I^2=98.46%, tau^2=1.35
- Group difference without outliers: g=0.47 (95% CI [0.28, 0.67])
- Pre-post difference: g=0.12 (95% CI [0.17, 0.41], p>0.05, k=23); bias-corrected: g=0.02 (95% CI [-0.12, 0.15])

**Competence:**
- Group difference: g=0.80 (initial, 95% CI [-0.49, 1.65], p>0.05, k=17); bias-corrected: g=0.48 (95% CI [0.17, 0.79], p<0.01, k=16), I^2=98.58%
- Pre-post difference: g=0.58 (95% CI [0.01, 1.15], p<0.05, k=9); outlier-removed: g=0.21 (95% CI [0.1, 0.32], p<0.05, k=8), I^2=94.78%

**Relatedness:**
- Group difference: g=1.66 (95% CI [-0.51, 3.83], p>0.05, k=12); outlier-removed: g=0.44 (95% CI [-0.14, 1.02]), I^2=99.64%
- Pre-post difference: g=1.43 (95% CI [-0.23, 3.09], p>0.05, k=10), I^2=99.65%

**Heterogeneity note**: I^2 values throughout are extremely high (94-99%), indicating massive variability across studies. The pooled estimates should be treated as directional indicators, not precise predictions.

**Publication bias**: Funnel plot asymmetry detected for intrinsic motivation group differences (Egger's z=4.15, p<0.0001) and competence (z=3.05, p=0.0023) and intrinsic motivation pre-post (z=3.09, p=0.002). Trim-and-fill corrections applied and reported above. No bias detected for autonomy support group differences.

## Mechanisms & Moderators

**Mechanisms (SDT framework):**
- Three basic psychological needs form the causal pathway: autonomy (sense of ownership/choice), competence (sense of capability), and relatedness (sense of belonging/connection)
- Supporting these needs is hypothesized to move students along the motivation continuum from amotivation → external regulation → introjected regulation → identified regulation → integrated regulation → intrinsic motivation
- Interventions failing to address all three needs may have weakened effects: "If students' needs for competence and relatedness were not successfully satisfied, then the intervention effects on intrinsic motivation could be weakened"

**Moderator: Duration of intervention**
- Group differences: interventions lasting 6 months to 1 year were most effective for autonomy support (b=11.23, 95% CIs [7.90, 14.56], p<0.000) and competence (b=8.69, 95% CIs [7.25, 10.14], p<0.000)
- Relatedness: interventions from 1 day to 1 month were best (b=8.25, 95% CIs [8.21, 8.91], p<0.000)
- Pre-post differences: interventions lasting 1 day to 1 month most effective for intrinsic motivation (b=0.67, 95% CIs [0.11, 1.24], p<0.02); interventions less than 1 day most effective for autonomy and relatedness

**Moderator: Student age**
- Ages 13-18 most effective for autonomy support (b=12.13, 95% CIs [9.35, 14.91], p<0.000) and competence (b=22.55, 95% CIs [19.10, 25.99], p<0.000)
- Ages 7-12 most effective for relatedness
- Adults (18+) most effective for relatedness pre-post (b=5.39, 95% CIs [5.04, 5.74], p<0.000)
- No significant age differences for intrinsic motivation across any comparison

**Moderator: Student gender**
- Female participants: interventions more effective for autonomy support (b=4.75, 95% CIs [2.78, 6.72], p<0.001) and competence (b=4.44, 95% CIs [3.67, 5.20], p<0.001) in group comparisons
- Relatedness: insufficient data for gender subgroup

**Moderator: Geographic origin**
- Europe: most effective for autonomy support (b=8.34, 95% CIs [6.18, 10.51], p<0.000) and competence (b=17.64, 95% CIs [14.93, 20.35], p<0.001) in group comparisons
- Asia: most effective for relatedness group differences and pre-post differences
- No continent differences for intrinsic motivation

**Moderator: Intervention approach**
- Moderator effects of intervention approach (teacher-centered, student-centered, parent-centered, mentor-mentee, combined) were not significant for any outcome — approach type did not reliably predict effectiveness

**Moderator: Study design**
- Insufficient data to estimate a moderator effect for study design (full-rank matrix requirement not met)

## Study Design Details

- **36 studies included** in systematic review; **31 in meta-analysis** (137 effect sizes, N=9,433)
- **Study designs**: experimental (RCT), quasi-experimental, and pre-post test designs; no pure qualitative designs in meta-analysis
- **Intervention types**: teacher-centered (n=14 articles, most common), student-centered (n=13), parent-centered (n=3), mentor-mentee (n=2), combined (n=4)
- **Age distribution**: 13 studies with ages 13-18; 12 with ages 7-12; 7 with young adults (18+); 4 not reported
- **Geographic distribution**: Europe (n=14), Asia (n=10), North America (n=10), South America (n=2)
- **Duration range**: 30 min (shortest, Patall et al., 2022) to 3 years (longest, Toshie & Osamu, 2017); most studies 1 month to 1 year
- **Effect size metric**: Hedges' g (SMD), calculated via metafor package in R with random effects models and robust variance estimation (RVE) for non-independent effect sizes
- **Interrater reliability**: 95.74% agreement rate; Cohen's Kappa k=0.84 (95% CI [0.57, 0.93])
- **Risk of bias**: Most studies did not report allocation concealment, blinding of outcome assessment, or blinding of participants — rated as unclear risk on most criteria. Most reported short-term effects only (24 studies did not include long-term data beyond 6 weeks).

## Limitations

- **No grey literature search**: Dissertation databases included but no forward-citing or grey literature systematic search — possible publication bias particularly for intrinsic motivation and competence outcomes
- **Extreme heterogeneity throughout**: I^2=94-99% across all outcomes. Pooled effect sizes are averages across wildly divergent studies; prediction intervals routinely span zero and large positive values. The heterogeneity undermines the meaningfulness of any single pooled estimate.
- **Short-term effects dominant**: 24 of 36 studies did not measure effects beyond 6 weeks. No evidence of durability of SDT interventions.
- **No race/ethnicity subgroup analysis**: Few studies reported participant race/ethnicity; demographic subgroup analyses for race could not be conducted.
- **Measurement heterogeneity**: Studies used different instruments (LCQ, SRQ, IMI and others) and measured different motivation subtypes (some only intrinsic, some full continuum). Table S1 lists all tools — not reproduced here.
- **Relatedness findings unreliable**: Only k=6 studies in competence subgroup and k=8 for relatedness subgroup in moderator analysis — too few for reliable moderator conclusions on these outcomes
- **Mostly K-12/classroom populations**: Majority of studies are teacher-training interventions in school settings. Minimal university-level or adult self-directed learning data.
- **Voluntary participation bias**: Most participants (teachers, parents, students) participated voluntarily — results may not generalize to less-motivated populations
- **No long-term sustainability assessment**: Most interventions (24 articles) did not assess long-term effects or involve participants in the intervention development process

---

## Population Scope Assessment

- **Population**: Mixed — primarily K-12 school students (ages 7-18) and their teachers/parents; 7 studies with university/young adult samples
- **Relevance to Tyler's context (adult async video)**: Medium-Low for intervention designs; Medium-High for motivational mechanisms
- **Red flags for direct transfer**: Almost all included interventions involve live, interactive, multi-session teacher-student interactions. Tyler's YouTube tutorials are one-directional and async — the autonomy-supportive *design principles* (offering choice, non-controlling language, optimal challenge, positive feedback) translate; the intervention formats do not.
- **Useful signal for Tyler**: The consistent finding that autonomy and competence support are the mechanisms that move the needle — not relatedness — has direct implications for tutorial design (choice architecture, acknowledging viewer competence, avoiding condescension, providing optimal challenge level).

---

## Conclusions

"Research on SDT interventions has documented their effectiveness and is a topic of growing interest... nine practical directions and recommendations for research and practice advice were provided to guide future SDT intervention design." The meta-analysis demonstrates that SDT-based educational interventions reliably improve perceived autonomy support (large effect, bias-corrected g=0.58-1.14 depending on comparison type) and show medium pre-post effects on competence (g=0.21-0.58 depending on outlier removal). Intrinsic motivation shows a partially significant group advantage (bias-corrected g=0.58) but no reliable pre-post growth. Relatedness effects are non-significant and inconsistent. Substantial heterogeneity across all outcomes requires caution in applying any single pooled estimate to specific contexts.

## Named Frameworks (if applicable)

- **Self-Determination Theory (SDT)**
  - Components: Autonomy (sense of ownership/choice in learning), Competence (sense of capability and efficacy), Relatedness (sense of belonging and connection)
  - Motivation taxonomy: Amotivation → External regulation → Introjected regulation → Identified regulation → Integrated regulation → Intrinsic motivation
  - Intervention strategies mapped to needs (from systematic review):
    - Autonomy support: offering choices, using non-controlling language, acknowledging perspectives, providing rationale
    - Competence support: positive feedback, optimal challenges, well-structured materials, clear goals
    - Relatedness support: inclusion, belonging, peer connection (least studied, least effective in this meta-analysis)

## Extraction Notes

**For Tyler's guidebook:** The most actionable signal from this paper is the hierarchy of SDT needs in practice: autonomy > competence >> relatedness for measurable intervention effects. In tutorial design terms: giving viewers a sense of control (choice of depth, navigation, pacing) and building genuine competence (accurate difficulty calibration, positive feedback loops) are the high-leverage SDT levers. Relatedness — the sense that "this creator cares about me and I belong in this learning community" — shows promise in theory but non-significant effects in this meta-analysis, possibly because it is harder to operationalize and less systematically targeted.

**Inflation flag note**: No inflation correction applied here — this is an educational meta-analysis with already-corrected (trim-and-fill) effect sizes reported. However, the dramatic reduction from initial to bias-corrected estimates (intrinsic motivation: g=0.96 → g=0.58) signals meaningful publication bias that was addressed by the authors. The bias-corrected figures are the appropriate values to cite.

**Compare with P012 (Howard)**: Howard (2021) is a broader SDT meta-analysis across all educational contexts. Wang et al. (2024) overlaps but focuses specifically on intervention studies (not correlational work) and explicitly tests autonomy/competence/relatedness as separate outcomes — making it more actionable for design guidance. Use Wang for "what interventions work," Howard for "why SDT matters overall."

**Compare with P037 (Rosli — SDT online)**: Wang et al. has minimal online/digital delivery coverage — only a handful of student-centered online studies. The digital-delivery modifier from P037 should be checked against these effect sizes.

**Quality note**: This is a well-executed meta-analysis with PRISMA protocol, RVE for non-independent effects, trim-and-fill correction, sensitivity analyses, and explicit outlier testing. The extreme heterogeneity (I^2=94-99%) is the main limitation — it reflects genuine diversity in SDT interventions, populations, and contexts rather than methodological failure.
