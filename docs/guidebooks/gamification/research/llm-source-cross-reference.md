## 5. Coverage Gaps

Areas where the LLM reports emphasize topics that our existing 54-source inventory appears weak or silent on.

### 5.1 Overjustification / Reward Undermining (CRITICAL GAP)

All three LLM reports place **Deci 1971**, **Lepper et al. 1973**, and especially the **Deci, Koestner, & Ryan 1999 meta-analysis** (128 studies) at the center of the "when gamification fails" story. Our inventory has zero sources from the overjustification/extrinsic reward literature. This is arguably the most important gap — the d = −0.40 effect for engagement-contingent rewards is a first-order design constraint that every chapter touching on reward mechanics needs to address.

**Recommended additions:** Deci 1971, Lepper et al. 1973, Deci et al. 1999 (all three).

### 5.2 Behavioral Economics Foundations

Our inventory includes Kivetz 2006 (goal-gradient) and Nunes 2006 (endowed progress) but is missing the foundational behavioral economics sources that explain *why* these mechanics work:
- **Kahneman & Tversky 1979** (Prospect Theory / loss aversion) — the engine behind streaks
- **Zeigarnik 1927** — incomplete task tension driving progress bars

Both LLMs cite these as mechanistic bedrock. Without them, the guidebook would be describing effects without explaining causes.

### 5.3 Variable Reward Schedules & Addiction Ethics

Our inventory has no sources on variable reinforcement schedules or their ethical implications. Both LLMs devote significant space to:
- **Skinner's operant conditioning** (schedules of reinforcement)
- **Loot box research** (Drummond & Sauer 2018; Brooks & Clark 2021 meta-analysis; Hing et al. 2022)
- **Social media addiction mechanics** (Clark & Zack 2023)

This gap matters because the CLAUDE.md explicitly requires: "Handle addiction mechanics carefully — acknowledge the overlap between engagement and exploitation" and "Dark patterns analyzed critically." We need sources to support this.

### 5.4 Landers' Empirical Work (Element-Mediator-Outcome)

We have Landers_2014 (theory paper) but NOT:
- **Landers & Landers 2014** (the empirical test with leaderboards — 29.61 more interactions)
- **Landers et al. 2017** (leaderboard goal-setting experiment)

Both LLMs treat Landers' empirical papers as essential — they provide the clearest causal evidence for *how* a specific mechanic works through a specific mediator. The theory paper alone is incomplete without the empirical validation.

### 5.5 Health Domain — Long-Duration RCTs

Our health evidence (Johnson 2016, Mazeas 2022, Nishi 2024) is entirely review/meta-analytic. LLM2/3 highlights:
- **Patel et al. 2021** — 1-year RCT with social mechanic comparison (support vs competition vs collaboration)
- **Althoff et al. 2016** — Pokémon GO physical activity data

These provide primary empirical evidence that the meta-analyses summarize.

### 5.6 Dark Patterns & Ethics Frameworks

Our inventory has no dedicated ethics sources. LLMs cite:
- **Kim & Werbach 2016** (four ethical dimensions of gamification)
- **Bogost 2011** ("exploitationware" critique)
- **Gray et al. 2018** (dark patterns in design practice)
- **Mathur et al. 2019** (dark patterns at scale)
- **Oinas-Kukkonen & Harjumaa 2009** (Persuasive Systems Design)
- **Fogg 2002** (Persuasive Technology book)

Given the CLAUDE.md requirement that "Ethics [be] woven throughout chapters," this is a structural gap.

### 5.7 Streak-Specific Research

Our inventory includes Silverman 2023 and Weathers 2024 on streaks, but LLMs surface additional streak evidence:
- **van Essen & Van Ouytsel 2023** — Snapchat streaks and problematic smartphone use
- **Carr & Rosaen 2024** — streaks degrading relational quality
- **Lally et al. 2010** — 66-day habit formation (missing a day doesn't matter — directly contradicts harsh streak design)

These complement our existing streak sources with harm evidence and habit science.

### 5.8 Habit Formation Science

No habit formation sources exist in our inventory. LLMs cite:
- **Lally et al. 2010** (66-day median to automaticity)
- **Judah et al. 2018** (intrinsic reward accelerates habit formation)

These are critical for the temporal dynamics chapter and for understanding when gamification scaffolding can be faded.

### 5.9 Workplace/Enterprise Gamification

Our inventory has no workplace-specific sources. LLM2 cites negative findings:
- **Mollick & Rothbard 2014** — mandatory gamification harmed performance
- **Rapp et al. 2018** — surveillance anxiety in enterprise gamification
- **Thom et al. 2012** — removing gamification caused contribution drops

While workplace is not our primary domain, cross-applicable findings about mandatory vs voluntary gamification and removal effects are relevant.

### 5.10 Narrative/Quest Mechanics

Our inventory has no sources specifically on narrative effectiveness in gamification. LLMs cite mixed evidence:
- **Sýkora et al. 2020** — decorative narrative had no effect (null finding)
- **Malone 1981** — fantasy as intrinsic motivation category

This gap means our guidebook's quest/narrative chapter would lack primary evidence.
