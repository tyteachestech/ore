# On or Off Track: How (Broken) Streaks Affect Consumer Decisions

## Study Overview
- **Citation**: Silverman, J. & Barasch, A., 2023
- **Study Type**: Multi-study experimental (7 studies: 1 correlational field study + 6 controlled experiments)
- **Sample**: n=980 (Study 1, field); n=~400–805 per lab study; total lab participants across Studies 2–7 ~3,800+; pilot n=100
- **DOI**: 10.1093/jcr/ucac029
- **PMID**: Not available

## Key Findings

- **Finding 1 (Field replication — Step-count data)**: Fitness app users were significantly more likely to meet a daily step challenge (7,000+ steps) when they had an intact logged streak versus a broken logged streak.
  - Effect: b = 0.38, SE = .03, Z = 11.27, p < .001 (intact vs. broken contrast)
  - Independent effects: Intact streak b = 0.25 (Z = 5.06, p < .001); Broken streak b = -1.01 (Z = -14.94, p < .001)
  - Sample: n = 980 university employees, 30-day challenge, correlational design

- **Finding 2 (Causal — Exercise domain)**: Holding actual behavior constant, participants shown a broken streak in their behavioral log were significantly less likely to choose the target behavior (another strength exercise) than those shown an intact streak.
  - Effect: 66.23% (intact) vs. 57.86% (broken) continued with strength exercise; X²(1) = 4.47, p = .035, OR = 1.43
  - Sample: n = 601 MTurk

- **Finding 3 (Bidirectional logging effect — Language learning)**: Logging amplified the streak effect in both directions. An intact logged streak strongly boosted target behavior (vs. no log), while a broken logged streak suppressed it (vs. no log).
  - Intact streak: 92.47% (log present) vs. 64.94% (log absent); X²(1) = 33.47, p < .001, OR = 6.63
  - Broken streak: 45.21% (log present) vs. 60.90% (log absent); X²(1) = 7.46, p = .006, OR = 0.53
  - Sample: n = 602 MTurk; 2 (streak: intact/broken) × 2 (log: present/absent) design

- **Finding 4 (Mechanism — Streak maintenance as a goal)**: Participants with an intact logged streak more explicitly reported that maintaining the streak was an active goal, and this mediated the effect on subsequent behavior.
  - Goal adoption: Intact M = 7.24 (SD = 3.38) vs. Broken M = 6.41 (SD = 3.22); t(398) = 2.52, p = .012, d = 0.25
  - Sense of accomplishment: Intact M = 7.49 (SD = 2.71) vs. Broken M = 6.51 (SD = 2.55); t(398) = 3.71, p < .001, d = 0.37
  - Mediation (streak goal → behavior): Indirect effect = 0.20, SE = 0.08, 95% CI [0.05, 0.37]
  - Mediation (accomplishment → behavior): Indirect effect = 0.31, SE = 0.09, 95% CI [0.15, 0.52]
  - Behavioral choice (intact vs. broken): 65.84% vs. 47.98%; X²(1) = 13.02, p < .001, OR = 2.09
  - No log condition fell between: 54.73% continued; intact vs. no-log: X²(1) = 5.20, p = .023, OR = 1.59
  - Sample: n = 601 MTurk; participants gave up watching a fun video clip to maintain streak

- **Finding 5 (Attribution moderates broken-streak effect)**: Self-attribution of a streak break drastically reduced subsequent engagement, more than externally-attributed breaks.
  - Intact: 53.17% continued target behavior
  - Externally-attributed broken: 42.00%; vs. intact: X²(1) = 3.43, p = .064, OR = 1.57
  - Self-attributed broken: 28.87%; vs. intact: X²(1) = 16.39, p < .001, OR = 2.80; vs. external: X²(1) = 5.48, p = .019, OR = 0.56
  - Sample: n = 418 MTurk (from n = 802 recruited)

- **Finding 6 (Streak repair attenuates drop-off)**: Giving users the ability to repair a broken streak (restore it to 4-in-a-row) substantially increased their likelihood of continuing the target behavior.
  - Intact: 93.14% continued; Broken: 68.66%; Repairable broken: 85.20%
  - Intact vs. Broken: X²(1) = 39.41, p < .001, OR = 6.20
  - Repairable vs. Broken: X²(1) = 15.26, p < .001, OR = 2.63
  - Intact vs. Repairable: X²(1) = 6.56, p = .010, OR = 2.36 (repair did not fully close the gap)
  - Sample: n = 601 MTurk

- **Finding 7 (Streak willingness-to-pay proxy)**: ~45% of participants were willing to watch an advertisement to maintain or repair their logged streak (Study 6). Streak motivation is strong enough to incur unrelated costs.

## Mechanisms & Moderators

- **Primary mechanism — Streak as goal**: Consumers adopt "maintaining my streak" as an independent goal. Intact streaks activate goal pursuit; broken streaks trigger goal failure responses (discouragement, lower self-efficacy). Negative emotions (sadness, anger) did NOT mediate the effect — sense of accomplishment did.
  - Evidence: Direct goal measure mediated behavioral choice (Study 4); sense of accomplishment mediated in Studies 4, 5, and 7; negative emotion did not mediate in Studies 5 or 7

- **Moderator 1 — Attribution of break**: Self-attribution drastically amplifies the negative effect of a broken streak. Externally-attributed breaks are less damaging.
  - Evidence: Self-attributed broken streak → 28.87% continued; externally attributed → 42.00%; intact → 53.17% (Study 6)

- **Moderator 2 — Streak repair opportunity**: Offering users a pathway to repair (fill in) a broken streak significantly attenuates the drop in engagement, though does not fully eliminate the gap vs. intact.
  - Evidence: Repairable broken: 85.20% continued vs. broken: 68.66% (Study 7, OR = 2.63)

- **Moderator 3 — Behavioral log presence (logging itself)**: Highlighting a streak (intact or broken) via a log amplifies effects in both directions relative to no log. Without a log, a broken streak is less demotivating; an intact streak is less motivating.
  - Evidence: Study 3 bidirectional interaction, F(1, 598) = 35.02, p < .001

- **Non-moderator — Streak length**: Effect of intact vs. broken streak is consistent regardless of streak length (3 vs. 6 vs. 20 consecutive behaviors). No significant interaction found (ps > .40).

- **Potential moderator — Public vs. private log**: Public logging directionally strengthened streak effects (OR = 1.72 public vs. OR = 1.52 private) but the interaction was not significant (F(1, 600) = 0.14, p = .71). Needs further study.

- **Potential moderator — Logging effort**: More active (manual) logging may strengthen streak identification as a self-signal. Not directly tested.

## Study Design Details

- **Intervention**: Behavioral logging app (modeled on Duolingo, MyFitnessPal, gaming apps) displaying checkmarks for streak-contributing behaviors; broken streaks displayed as an "X" or quota message
- **Control/Comparison**: Intact streak vs. broken streak; many studies include a no-log control condition
- **Streak break operationalization**: App error preventing logging; "quota" message (externally attributed); narrow categorization (only one behavior type counts); failing a difficult task (self-attributed)
- **Domains tested**: Step counts (field), strength exercise, language learning (Portuguese), word/number games
- **All DV**: Real behavioral choices (not hypothetical) — continue target behavior vs. switch or stop
- **Power**: Studies 2–7 powered for ≥80% power at anticipated effect sizes (n = 400–800 per study); Studies 4–7 preregistered
- **Participant source**: MTurk (lab studies), university employees (field study)
- **Data/materials**: OSF repository https://osf.io/kpjh9/

## Limitations

- **Field study is correlational**: Study 1 cannot rule out self-selection confounds (people with intact streaks may differ systematically from those without)
- **Lab samples (MTurk)**: Most studies used MTurk adults; generalizability to less tech-savvy, older, or clinical populations is unknown
- **Short-horizon measurement**: Behavioral DVs measured immediately after streak manipulation, not longitudinally; unclear if streak motivation persists over weeks/months
- **Streak length ceiling untested**: Studied up to 20-length streaks; effects for very long streaks (e.g., 100+ days, as in Duolingo power users) remain unknown — satiation may eventually dominate
- **Negative emotions not a mechanism**: The paper demonstrates sense of accomplishment (not negative emotion) mediates the effect, which partially conflicts with intuitive "frustration" explanations; this nuance deserves replication
- **Repair did not fully close the gap**: Repairable broken streaks improved engagement but did not match intact streaks, suggesting residual "authenticity" valuation that is not yet theorized
- **No conflict of interest disclosure**: Partial funding from Wharton's Baker Retail Center; no stated conflicts, but commercial relevance is high
- **Public log moderation underpowered**: The public vs. private manipulation in Study S4 was not designed to test that specific interaction and was borderline non-significant

## Population Scope Assessment

- **Population**: General adult consumers (MTurk workers, university employees)
- **Relevance to casual behavior change**: High — directly tests apps used for habit building (fitness, language learning, gaming), all common consumer contexts
- **Red flags**: None. No clinical populations; no addiction context. MTurk skews younger and more tech-familiar than general population but is not a pathological sample.

## Conclusions

Across seven studies, intact logged streaks increase and broken logged streaks decrease subsequent engagement in the same behavior, even when actual past behavior is held constant — the log representation alone drives the effect. The mechanism is that consumers adopt streak maintenance as an independent goal; sense of accomplishment (not negative emotion) mediates motivation. The effect is worsened by self-attribution of the break and partially reversed by streak repair opportunities. As the authors note: "Consumers consider maintaining a logged streak to be a meaningful goal in and of itself."

## Named Frameworks (if applicable)

- **Streaks as Goals Framework** (authors' own)
  - Components: Behavioral log → streak salience → streak maintenance goal adoption → subsequent behavioral engagement
  - Key boundary: Effect operates independently of actual behavior; representation in the log is what matters
  - Research alignment:
    - Intact streak → goal activation (consistent with Bandura self-efficacy literature)
    - Broken streak → goal failure response (consistent with Soman & Cheema 2004, Heath et al. 1999)
    - Repair → goal resumption (consistent with Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014 fresh-start research)

## Extraction Notes

This is one of the most directly applicable papers in the gamification domain for NotionRPG. Key takeaways for design:

1. **Streak visibility is a double-edged sword**: Showing broken streaks can backfire. NotionRPG should consider whether surfacing broken streaks prominently is wise, or whether to suppress them/reframe them.
2. **The "repair" finding is actionable**: Offering users a way to retroactively restore a broken streak (e.g., by completing a makeup task) materially rescues engagement (OR = 2.63 for repairable vs. broken). This is a concrete mechanic worth building.
3. **Self-attribution is the danger zone**: When users feel personally responsible for a streak break (vs. blaming the system), drop-off is severe. App design should lean toward attributing breaks to external factors when possible.
4. **Broad category definitions help**: Defining what "counts" more broadly (e.g., any habit activity, not just the exact one) keeps more users on-streak. Study 5 demonstrates this cleanly.
5. **Streak length doesn't matter much for the basic effect** (at least up to 20 consecutive behaviors): Don't over-engineer early streak length thresholds.
6. The effect sizes across 6+ studies are consistent and moderate-to-large (ORs ranging 1.43 to 6.20 depending on context), with field replication. Evidence quality is solid for a consumer behavior paper.
