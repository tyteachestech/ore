# The Goal-Gradient Hypothesis Resurrected: Purchase Acceleration, Illusionary Goal Progress, and Customer Retention

## Study Overview

- **Citation**: Kivetz, R., Urminsky, O., & Zheng, Y. (2006)
- **Study Type**: Multi-study field experimental / secondary data analysis
- **Sample**: Multiple studies totaling ~1,464+ participants and observations
  - Café RP (main): 949 completed 10-stamp cards (~10,000 coffee purchases, 29,076 data rows)
  - Illusionary progress field experiment: n=108 café customers (randomized)
  - Illusionary progress process test 1: n=65 train station travelers
  - Illusionary progress process test 2 (sunk cost): n=118 travelers
  - Jaboom music-rating program: 148 members, 14,866 songs rated, 472 site visits
  - Frequent-flyer promotion sensitivity study: n=329 respondents
  - Retention/reengagement analysis: n=110 members with 2 completed cards
- **Publication**: Journal of Marketing Research, Vol. XLIII (February 2006), pp. 39–58
- **DOI**: 10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39
- **Context**: Two real reward programs — a university campus café loyalty program and an online music-rating incentive system (Jaboom/MoodLogic)

## Key Findings

- **Finding 1 — Purchase acceleration (café RP)**: Members accelerated coffee purchases as they approached the 10-stamp reward. Raw mean interpurchase times decreased by 0.7 days from first to last stamp (t = 2.6, p < .05), a 20% acceleration. Card completion averaged 24.6 days versus an estimated 29.4 days had purchases continued at the initial rate — a 4.8-day (16%) difference.

- **Finding 2 — Hazard rate model confirms acceleration**: Discrete-time proportional hazard rate GDM (controlling for weekly stamp volume, seasonality, day-of-week, card type, and latent heterogeneity across 5 classes): linear goal-distance parameter b1 = −0.03 (p < .01); quadratic b2 = −0.04 (p < .01). Nested LRT: χ² = 18.8, df = 2, p < .01 over null model. Effect held after modeling unobserved heterogeneity in both hazard rate and acceleration parameters (largest segment = 58%).

- **Finding 3 — Illusionary goal progress (field experiment)**: Customers randomly assigned to 12-stamp card with 2 pre-filled bonus stamps (illusionary progress group, same absolute remaining distance = 10 stamps) completed the program in 12.7 days vs. 15.6 days for the 10-stamp control group — 2.9 days faster, 20% reduction (t = 2.0, p < .05; Mann-Whitney U medians: 10 vs. 15 days, Z = 2.1, p < .05). Sunk-cost alternative ruled out: respondents lost the pre-filled card felt no worse than those who lost an unfilled card (sunk-cost > illusionary-progress in negative affect, t = 1.9, p < .05).

- **Finding 4 — Illusionary progress (questionnaire)**: Train station travelers estimated completing an 8-meal pizza program in 11 weeks (10-stamp card with 2 bonus stamps framing) vs. 16 weeks (8-stamp baseline framing) (t = 1.6, p < .1; median test: 8 vs. 12 weeks, χ² = 6.5, p < .05). No difference in likelihood of joining the program (5.9 vs. 5.8 on 0–10 scale), ruling out idiosyncratic fit explanation.

- **Finding 5 — Post-reward resetting**: Members completing two sequential cards showed identical acceleration curves on each card, with interpurchase times resetting to first-card start levels between cards. First two times on Card 2 (3.1, 2.7 days) matched first two times on Card 1 (3.2, 2.8 days) and were significantly slower than last two times on Card 1 (2.2, 2.1 days) (p < .01). This pattern rules out habituation and time-trend explanations.

- **Finding 6 — No acceleration without reward (transparent cards)**: Control customers carrying unredeemable transparent cards showed deceleration (GDM b1 = +0.3, p < .01), confirming that acceleration was driven by goal motivation not time-trend.

- **Finding 7 — Music-rating: intervisit times**: Jaboom members visited more frequently as goal approached. Hazard rate GDM: b1 = −0.25 (p < .01), b2 = −0.22 (p < .01). Pattern replicated even after unobserved heterogeneity modeling (two-class: 65% with b1 = −0.6, 35% with b1 = −0.1). Incomplete certificates showed deceleration (b1 = +0.7, p < .1). Completion vs. non-completion interaction: bINT = −0.5, χ² = 16.2, p < .01.

- **Finding 8 — Music-rating: quantity per visit**: Tobit GDM of songs-per-visit on completed certificates: b1 = −13.0 (p < .01), b2 = −7.5 (p < .01). Post-reward resetting: last visit of first certificate averaged 24 songs vs. 16 songs on first visit of second certificate (pairwise t = 2.3, p < .05).

- **Finding 9 — Music-rating: effort persistence**: Logit GDM of visit termination probability: b1 = +0.49 (p < .01) — greater goal distance predicted greater likelihood of quitting. 17% (80/473) of all visit terminations occurred immediately after reward attainment (at dt = 1.0); only 0.2% (1/473) occurred when 50 songs already accumulated (dt = .02).

- **Finding 10 — Retention prediction**: Individual-level first-card acceleration predicted both retention probability (logistic b = −0.5, Wald χ² = 5.3, p < .05) and reengagement time (Weibull hazard b = −2.6, Wald χ² = 13.8, p < .01), controlling for overall purchase rate and card completion date.

- **Finding 11 — Promotion sensitivity**: Near-goal respondents (23,000/25,000 miles accumulated) accepted a bonus-miles promotional offer at 56% vs. 38% for distant-goal respondents (13,000/25,000 miles), χ² = 10.7, p < .001.

## Mechanisms & Moderators

- **Core mechanism — proportional goal distance**: The key predictor of motivation is *dt = (r – nt)/r*, the proportion of original distance remaining. Absolute remaining distance is NOT the driver — demonstrated by the illusionary progress results, which held absolute distance constant while manipulating proportional distance only.

- **Mechanism — diminishing acceleration rate**: Goal gradient curves show diminishing acceleration (negative quadratic term) consistent with Hull's (1934) animal data. Effort ramps up gradually rather than sharply at the end.

- **Moderator — reward contingency**: Acceleration is entirely absent (reversed to deceleration) when no reward is contingent on completion (transparent card control), confirming the effect is goal-motivational rather than habitual.

- **Moderator — individual differences in acceleration slope**: Steeper individual goal gradients predict higher retention and faster reengagement. Gradient slope is interpretable as a measure of reward motivation strength.

- **Moderator — post-reward reset**: Effort systematically drops after goal attainment and re-accelerates toward the next goal. Highest defection risk occurs immediately after reward is earned (when dt returns to 1.0).

- **Boundary condition — task type**: Effect generalizes across purchase timing, effort quantity, and effort persistence — three distinct operationalizations across two very different programs (café purchases vs. online music rating labor).

- **Mechanism — illusionary progress is not sunk cost**: Pre-filled stamps do not feel like sunk cost (no greater loss aversion when losing the card). Effect is purely the perceived proportional head start.

## Study Design Details

- **Café RP Design**: Naturalistic field study. Customers enrolled in real loyalty card program. Sequential stamps purchased at real café, tracked via sequential numbering on stamps. Three groups: (1) main redeemers (n=949 completed cards), (2) buyback incomplete cards (n=73), (3) transparent/unredeemable control (n=42). Duration of observations: natural program participation.
- **Illusionary progress field experiment**: True RCT; research assistants randomly assigned customers at café to 10-stamp vs. 12-stamp-with-2-bonus-stamps cards (n=108).
- **Illusionary progress process tests**: Paper-and-pencil questionnaires with train station travelers; sunk-cost test used 3-condition between-subjects design (n=118).
- **Jaboom program**: Secondary data from real incentive program run by MoodLogic Inc.; 148 members, 14,866 songs, 472 visits; 24-month ongoing program (expiration ruled out).
- **Statistical methods**: Discrete-time proportional hazard rate model (logistic parameterization); Tobit model (effort quantity); logit model (effort persistence); latent class segmentation for unobserved heterogeneity throughout.
- **Follow-up**: Retention tracked for subset who earned second reward (n=110).
- **Attrition/censoring**: Buyback cards sample collected for incomplete card data (otherwise only redeemed cards observed); incomplete certificates analyzed separately in music program.

## Limitations

- **Population**: All café participants were university campus customers, likely skewing younger and more habitual coffee drinkers than general adult population.
- **Self-selection**: Only redeemed cards are fully observed in the main café dataset. Buyback sample provides a window into non-redeemers but is not a random sample of all participants.
- **Single-location context**: Coffee and online music rating are specific, low-stakes reward contexts. Generalizability to higher-stakes or more cognitively demanding behavioral domains is unconfirmed.
- **No direct measure of motivation**: Psychological goal distance is inferred from behavioral data; latent motivation is not directly measured.
- **Illusionary progress boundary conditions**: The process by which bonus stamps are framed (e.g., "for everyone who joins" vs. personalized) matters for ruling out idiosyncratic fit. Boundary conditions under different framing not fully explored.
- **No long-term follow-up beyond two cards**: Customer retention analysis is limited to two sequential reward cycles, leaving longer-term dynamics unexplored.
- **Observational music data**: The Jaboom dataset is secondary; participants self-selected into the program.
- **No effect size standardization**: Key effects reported as raw model parameters (b coefficients) from hazard rate, Tobit, and logit models — not as standardized effect sizes (no Cohen's d). Comparisons across studies must be interpreted cautiously.

## Population Scope Assessment

- **Population**: General adult (university community café customers + internet users for music program)
- **Relevance to casual behavior change / gamification systems**: Very high. Both contexts (loyalty cards, online effort-reward programs) are direct analogues for gamification mechanics — streaks, progress bars, XP-to-reward systems, achievement unlocks.
- **Red flags**: None. No clinical population, no addiction context. The café coffee context has minor ecological validity limits (campus population, exclusive café), but the music-rating replication extends the finding broadly.

## Conclusions

The authors conclude that the goal-gradient hypothesis extends robustly from animal learning to the human psychology of rewards. "Not only do customers accelerate toward rewards (in terms of timing, quantity, and persistence of effort), but their acceleration also predicts loyalty and future engagement with similar goals." The GDM, parameterized as effort = f(proportional goal distance remaining), unifies all effects. The illusionary progress finding is particularly significant: perceived, not actual, goal progress is the causal mechanism, which rules out cost–benefit and time-discounting explanations. The authors recommend that reward programs (and incentive systems generally) leverage illusionary goal progress through preloaded bonus credits and attend to post-reward re-enrollment dynamics, as defection risk is highest immediately after reward attainment.

## Named Frameworks

- **Goal-Distance Model (GDM)**
  - Core formula: dt = (r – nt)/r, where r = total effort requirement, nt = effort accumulated at time t
  - Range: dt ∈ [0, 1] — 1 when no progress made, 0 when goal achieved
  - Effort investment modeled as function of dt (negative relationship: smaller dt → greater effort)
  - Operationalizations: hazard rate (timing), Tobit (quantity), logit (persistence)
  - Tested with: discrete-time proportional hazard model, Tobit model, logit model
  - Latent class extensions for unobserved heterogeneity in both base rates and acceleration tendency

## Quotable Passages

> "On average, interpurchase times decrease by 20% or .7 days throughout the program." (Abstract)

> "The illusion of progress toward the goal induces purchase acceleration. For example, customers who receive a 12-stamp coffee card with two preexisting 'bonus' stamps complete the ten required purchases faster than customers who receive a 'regular' 10-stamp card." (Abstract)

> "Members accelerated their coffee purchases toward their first reward (a free coffee) and then slowed down when they began working toward a second similar reward; the same members subsequently reaccelerated their purchases as they approached the second reward. These findings rule out the habituation account and other forms of time trend." (p. 46)

> "The 20% (.7 day) decrease in average interpurchase times from the first to the last stamp on the card implies that in a typical month, on average, members purchased two more coffees than they would have without an RP in order to earn one free coffee." (p. 47)

## Boundary Conditions & Population Caveats

- **Applies to**: Reward program structures where effort is discrete and trackable, consumers who opted into programs (self-selected motivated participants), general adult contexts, online effort-reward systems analogous to gamified apps
- **Does NOT apply to**: Reward structures where the goal is invisible or uncommunicated (no progress indicator), contexts where participants are unaware of their proximity to a goal, populations with no achievement motivation or no awareness of reward structure
- **Unknown/untested**: Whether the effect holds with progress bars vs. stamps specifically; whether digital-only (no physical card) implementations show equivalent magnitudes; long-term dynamics beyond two reward cycles; whether the illusionary progress boundary matters (e.g., staff-initiated vs. algorithm-generated bonus credits); effect of reward value magnitude on gradient steepness; comparison across reward program types (miles, cash back, free goods); whether intermittent vs. fixed-ratio reward schedules interact with the gradient

## Extraction Notes

This is one of the most cited and directly applicable papers in the gamification corpus. The methodology is rigorous for a field study — naturalistic behavior tracked longitudinally, with experimental controls (transparent cards, buyback), alternative explanations systematically eliminated (habituation, sunk cost, idiosyncratic fit, heterogeneity bias, time trends), and replication across two very different contexts.

Key design insights for NotionRPG:
1. **Progress bars work via proportional distance, not absolute distance** — displaying "80% complete" matters more than "4 of 5 remaining"
2. **Pre-filled progress (illusionary head start) accelerates completion by ~20%** — direct justification for "bonus XP on joining" or "day 1 streak credit" mechanics
3. **Post-reward resetting is the highest churn risk moment** — triggering re-enrollment prompts or "next challenge" framing immediately after reward attainment is critical
4. **Stronger accelerators = better retained users** — the goal gradient can serve as an engagement quality metric, not just a design variable
5. **Acceleration generalizes to quantity, not just timing** — XP/points earned per session may also show gradient effects

No standardized effect sizes (Cohen's d) are reported; all results are in raw model coefficients. This limits direct comparison with meta-analytic effect sizes but does not undermine internal validity. The field experiment approach provides strong causal evidence for the illusionary progress manipulation specifically.
