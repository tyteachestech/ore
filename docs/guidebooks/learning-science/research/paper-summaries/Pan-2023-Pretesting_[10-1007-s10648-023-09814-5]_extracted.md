# Prequestioning and Pretesting Effects: a Review of Empirical Research, Theoretical Perspectives, and Implications for Educational Practice

## Study Overview
- **Citation**: Pan, S. C., & Carpenter, S. K. (2023)
- **Study Type**: Systematic Review
- **Sample**: 60+ peer-reviewed studies on prequestioning and pretesting (modern literature: 2009-2023)
- **DOI**: 10.1007/s10648-023-09814-5
- **Publication**: Educational Psychology Review, 35, 97

## Key Findings

- **Finding 1: Pre-instruction testing benefits learning even when initial responses are incorrect**
  - Pretesting (taking tests before learning content) enhances subsequent learning of the correct answers across diverse materials (text passages, videos, lectures, word pairs, trivia facts)
  - Benefit persists despite participants generating many erroneous responses during practice testing ("errorful generation")

- **Finding 2: Effect sizes for memory of directly tested content are substantial**
  - Reported Cohen's d values range from 0.44 to well over 2.0 for memory improvements at retention intervals of 24h or longer
  - Effects observed with text passages (Richland et al., 2009; Little & Bjork, 2016), paired associates (Kornell et al., 2009; Pan et al., 2019), and trivia facts (Kornell, 2014)

- **Finding 3: Benefits persist across educationally meaningful retention intervals**
  - Classroom studies show effects lasting days to weeks: Beckman (2008) observed 9-12% better performance on end-of-unit tests; Soderstrom & Bjork (2023) found 8-9% better performance on high-stakes final exam several weeks later
  - Kliegl et al. (2022) found pretesting effects actually grew stronger with longer retention intervals (largest effect at 1-week delay)

- **Finding 4: Transfer benefits to untested content are inconsistent but possible**
  - Directly tested materials: consistent benefits across studies
  - Untested materials presented for study: mixed results (benefits in some studies with text passages, e.g., Little & Bjork 2016; no benefits in others, e.g., Richland et al. 2009 experiments 2-5)
  - Transfer to inferences and new exemplars: limited evidence but some success (Sana, Yan et al., 2020a, 2020b on statistical classification)

- **Finding 5: Test format (multiple-choice vs. cued recall) generally does not constrain benefits**
  - Pan & Sana (2021) found no substantial difference in effect magnitude between multiple-choice and cued recall pretests
  - Exception: semantically unrelated word pairs and foreign language vocabulary show benefits only on recognition posttests, not cued recall

- **Finding 6: Immediate feedback is critical for paired associate learning but not for semantically rich materials**
  - Paired associates (e.g., doctor-nurse): pretesting benefits require immediate correct answer feedback; delayed feedback nullifies advantage (Grimaldi & Karpicke 2012; Vaughn & Rawson 2012; Hays et al. 2013)
  - Trivia facts and text passages: benefits persist even with delayed feedback (24h+) (Kornell 2014)

- **Finding 7: Semantic association moderates effects for simple materials**
  - Weakly semantically-associated word pairs (e.g., mother-child) show pretesting benefits
  - Semantically unrelated pairs (e.g., door-shoe) show NO benefit with cued recall posttest (Grimaldi & Karpicke 2012; Huelser & Metcalfe 2012; Knight et al. 2012)

- **Finding 8: Learners hold metacognitive illusions about pretesting effectiveness**
  - Huelser & Metcalfe (2012): participants ranked pretesting as least effective despite pretested items being best remembered
  - Judgments of learning (JOLs): 78% rated reading as more effective than pretesting (Yang et al. 2017); 56-44 split on guess-and-study vs. study-only (Pan et al. 2020a, 2020b)
  - Metacognitive misperceptions can be corrected with explanations or performance feedback (Yang et al. 2017; Pan & Rivers 2023)

## Effect Sizes (Raw Values)

**Memory for directly tested content (consolidated from review):**
- Cohen's d range: 0.44 to 2.0+ (at 24h+ retention intervals)
- Classroom studies (percentage point improvements):
  - Beckman (2008): 9-12% better on unit tests
  - Soderstrom & Bjork (2023): 8-9% better on final exam
  - Carpenter et al. (2018): ~7% better on same-day posttest (decline at 1-week follow-up, not significant)

**Paired associates with immediate feedback:**
- Robust benefits with semantic association; effect sizes not explicitly quantified in review prose (see meta-analyses cited: King-Shepard et al., n.d.; St. Hilaire et al., 2023)

**Classroom implementations:**
- Video (Carpenter & Toftness 2017): benefit for directly tested content, consistent pattern replicated across 5+ studies
- Text passages: benefits replicated across multiple studies with various materials

## Mechanisms & Moderators

**Proposed Mechanisms for Paired Associates with Immediate Feedback:**
- **Mediator account**: Incorrect responses during practice testing generate mediator words (e.g., "father" for mother-child pair) that later serve as retrieval pathways to correct answers on posttest
- **Search set account**: Attempting a practice question activates a set of candidate answers; correct answer is already "activated" when presented as feedback, enhancing encoding
- **Attention focusing account**: Pretesting directs attention to correct answer feedback regardless of semantic association with cue

**Proposed Mechanisms for Text Passages and Rich Materials:**
- **Test-induced encoding changes**: Pretesting alters how learners search through, attend to, and encode subsequent learning materials
- **Activation of relevant knowledge**: Practice testing activates prior knowledge or contextual information, which then interacts with correct answer feedback

**Moderators/Boundary Conditions:**

| Factor | Effect | Evidence |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Semantic association (word pairs)** | Weak/absent association = no benefit with cued recall | Grimaldi & Karpicke 2012; Huelser & Metcalfe 2012 |
| **Timing of feedback (paired associates)** | Immediate > delayed (nullifies advantage) | Vaughn & Rawson 2012; Hays et al. 2013 |
| **Timing of feedback (rich materials)** | Delayed feedback still benefits (even 24h) | Kornell 2014 |
| **Retention interval length** | Longer intervals = larger effects | Kliegl et al. 2022 |
| **Test format (paired associates)** | Format (MC vs. cued recall) doesn't constrain benefit | Pan & Sana 2021 |
| **Posttest format (semantically unrelated vocab)** | Recognition > cued recall | Seabrooke et al. 2021a, 2021b |
| **Dropout/persistence (online)** | Pretesting may reduce persistence in MOOCs | Janelli & Lipnevich 2021 |

## Study Design Details

**Standard Laboratory Design:**
- Experimental group: (1) Attempt practice test questions on unlearned content, (2) Study/learn correct answers, (3) Take posttest
- Control group: Study target content without practice testing, (4) Take posttest
- Posttest timing: Immediate to several weeks
- Measures: "Old" questions (directly tested) vs. "New" questions (untested but available during study or transfer inferences)

**Common Variations:**
- **Separated phases**: Practice testing prior to learning opportunity (text/video/lecture materials)
- **Intermingled**: Immediate feedback after each practice question (paired associates, trivia)
- **Interpolated**: Repeated cycles of practice testing + learning across material segments (Carpenter & Toftness 2017; Pan et al. 2020a, 2020b)

**Classroom Implementations:**
- Pretests administered at unit/lecture start without feedback (Beckman 2008; Soderstrom & Bjork 2023)
- High-stakes posttest weeks later (end-of-unit or final exam)

## Limitations

**Stated/Obvious Limitations:**

- **Inconsistency in transfer effects**: While directly tested content shows consistent benefits, transfer to untested materials presented for study is highly mixed, making it unclear when and why pretesting supports generalization
- **Lack of evidence on higher-order outcomes**: Limited research on inference-drawing and classification transfer; only 2-3 studies address these outcomes
- **Metacognitive gap**: Learners consistently underestimate pretesting benefits and overestimate reading effectiveness (78% preference for reading), creating adoption barrier despite educational efficacy
- **Material constraints**: Benefits for semantically unrelated paired associates and foreign language vocabulary depend on posttest format (recognition only), limiting applicability
- **MOOC/dropout effects**: One classroom study (Janelli & Lipnevich 2021) found pretesting reduced persistence in online courses, suggesting motivation/engagement trade-off in self-paced settings
- **Predominantly undergraduate samples**: Most studies use undergraduate students; generalizability to younger ages (exception: Lima & Jaeger 2020 with 4th-5th graders) or older adults unclear
- **Short publication timeline**: Modern literature spans only ~15 years (2009-2023), limits confidence in long-term durability and institutional implementation patterns
- **Heterogeneity in procedures**: Spacing of feedback (immediate vs. delayed), interpolation of questions, material type, and test format vary widely across studies, making synthesis and meta-analysis challenging

**Unstated/Conceptual Limitations:**

- **Publication bias**: Not addressed in review; pretesting effects may be overestimated if failed replications remain unpublished
- **Effect sizes not uniform**: Range of 0.44 to 2.0+ in effect sizes across studies suggests important moderators remain unidentified
- **Mechanism theories immature**: Proposed accounts (mediator, search set, attention focusing) are relatively general and lack direct empirical testing (e.g., no mediator-word retrieval confirmation studies cited)
- **Limited exploration of failures**: Richland et al. (2009) showed transfer benefits in 1 of 5 experiments—review discusses but doesn't fully explain why conditions 2-5 failed

## Population Scope Assessment

- **Population**: Primarily undergraduate students in U.S./Western universities; limited data on K-12, older adults, clinical or clinical-adjacent populations
- **Relevance to casual behavior change**: **Medium-High** — Applies directly to learning and knowledge acquisition, which underpin intentional behavior change (e.g., learning healthy habits, understanding barriers). Less direct relevance to motivational or emotional behavior change (e.g., overcoming addiction, managing affect-driven impulses)
- **Red flags**:
  - One classroom study (Janelli & Lipnevich 2021) shows pretesting can reduce persistence in low-stakes online contexts, suggesting potential disengagement risk if misapplied to motivational contexts
  - No evidence on clinical populations (ADHD, learning disabilities, anxiety); applicability unclear
  - "Struggle first" framing in clinical skill training (Willis et al. 2020) may not translate to habit/behavior domains

## Conclusions

Pan (2023) concludes: "The evidence to date indicates that prequestioning and pretesting can often enhance learning, but the extent of that enhancement may vary due to differences in procedure or how learning is assessed. The underlying cognitive mechanisms, which can be represented by a three-stage framework, appear to involve test-induced changes in subsequent learning behaviors and possibly other processes. Further research is needed to clarify moderating factors, theoretical issues, and best practices for educational applications."

The review emphasizes that prequestioning and pretesting are viable, evidence-based learning strategies that challenge the conventional assumption that error-prone initial attempts are pedagogically fruitless. However, benefits are not universal across all materials, formats, and contexts, and learners' metacognitive illusions about these strategies present a significant adoption barrier despite their efficacy.

## Named Frameworks (if applicable)

- **Three-Stage Framework** (mentioned in abstract/conclusion but not fully detailed in readable sections):
  - Stage 1: Test-induced changes during practice testing
  - Stage 2: Altered learning behaviors during subsequent study
  - Stage 3: Integration or consolidation effects at posttest
  - **Research alignment**: Components 1-2 appear supported by attention-focusing and search-set mechanisms; Stage 3 mechanism less explicitly discussed in review excerpts

## Extraction Notes

This is a high-quality systematic review spanning 60+ modern studies (2009-2023) on a well-defined phenomenon. The paper excels at documenting boundary conditions and inconsistencies (e.g., why transfer sometimes works, sometimes doesn't), which strengthens its trustworthiness. Key value for NotionRPG: **prequestioning could scaffold behavior-change learning** (e.g., asking users "What would you do if tempted to break your habit?" before showing strategies), but evidence is strongest for academic materials, and metacognitive misperceptions may require additional motivational framing to overcome user resistance. The MOOC dropout finding (Janelli & Lipnevich 2021) is a notable caution for low-stakes digital implementation.
