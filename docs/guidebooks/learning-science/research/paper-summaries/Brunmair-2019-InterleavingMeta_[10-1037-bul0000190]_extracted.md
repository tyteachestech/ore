# Similarity Matters: A Meta-Analysis of Interleaved Learning and Its Moderators

## Study Overview
- **Citation**: Brunmair, M., & Richter, T. (2019)
- **Study Type**: Meta-analysis
- **Sample**: k=54 experiments from 38 articles; N=4,136 participants total
- **DOI**: 10.1037/bul0000190
- **PMID**: 31033317

**What was studied**: The interleaving effect — whether studying multiple categories in an interleaved (ABCABC) vs. blocked (AAABBBCCC) order improves category learning and inductive reasoning. The central question: does interleaving always help, and what moderates the effect?

---

## Key Findings

- **Finding 1 — Overall interleaving advantage (overall)**: Interleaved practice produces a moderate advantage over blocked practice on delayed test performance across all studies.
  - Hedges' g = 0.42 (95% CI [0.30, 0.54]), k=54
  - Heterogeneity: I² = 68.4%, τ² = 0.13 — substantial, indicating effect is not uniform

- **Finding 2 — Similarity is the critical moderator**: The interleaving advantage depends fundamentally on the within-category similarity (how alike items in one category are to each other) relative to between-category similarity. When categories are dissimilar from each other, blocking is as good or better. When categories share surface features but differ in deep structure, interleaving is most beneficial.
  - High discriminability condition (categories clearly distinguishable): g ≈ 0.15–0.20 (small, non-significant in some analyses)
  - Low discriminability condition (categories overlap): g ≈ 0.60–0.80 (large)

- **Finding 3 — Material type is the strongest practical moderator**:
  - Paintings/artwork: g = 0.67 (95% CI [0.47, 0.87]) — large benefit of interleaving
  - Mathematics/science problems: g = 0.34 (95% CI [0.16, 0.52]) — moderate benefit
  - Words/vocabulary: g = −0.39 (95% CI [−0.65, −0.13]) — blocking is BETTER
  - Birds/natural categories: g ≈ 0.50 (positive but wider CI)
  - Effect reported as significant (p < .05) for paintings; p < .01 for words (blocking superior)

- **Finding 4 — Test delay moderates the effect**: Longer delays between training and test produce larger interleaving advantages than immediate testing, consistent with interleaving helping with long-term retention specifically.
  - Immediate test: g ≈ 0.20–0.25
  - Delayed test (≥1 day): g ≈ 0.55–0.70

- **Finding 5 — Study phase performance**: Interleaving often produces worse study-phase performance (errors, slower processing) than blocking — but this desirable difficulty leads to better delayed test performance. The inversion of study-phase and test-phase performance is a hallmark signature.

- **Finding 6 — Publication bias minimal**: No evidence of significant publication bias detected via funnel plot asymmetry or Egger's test. The effect is considered robust to file-drawer concerns.

---

## Effect Sizes (Raw Values)

**Overall effect (interleaved vs. blocked):**
- g = 0.42 (95% CI [0.30, 0.54]), k=54 experiments, N≈4,136
- I² = 68.4%, τ² = 0.13
- Heterogeneity: substantial — material type explains a large portion

**By material type (primary moderator):**
- Paintings/artwork: g = 0.67 (95% CI [0.47, 0.87]), k=15
- Natural categories (birds, butterflies): g ≈ 0.50 (positive)
- Mathematics/STEM problem types: g = 0.34 (95% CI [0.16, 0.52]), k=16
- Words/vocabulary/foreign language: g = −0.39 (95% CI [−0.65, −0.13]), k=8 — blocking superior

**By test delay:**
- Immediate testing: g ≈ 0.20–0.25 (small)
- Delayed testing: g ≈ 0.55–0.70 (medium-large)
- Moderator effect: β for delay = positive and significant (p < .05)

**Discriminability:**
- High discriminability (distinct categories): smaller effect, approaches null
- Low discriminability (overlapping categories): larger effect, g ≈ 0.60+

**Publication bias:**
- Egger's test: non-significant — no evidence of bias
- Trim-and-fill: no adjustment required; original g = 0.42 stands

## Mechanisms & Moderators

**Primary mechanism — Discriminative contrast hypothesis**: Interleaving forces learners to compare adjacent items from different categories, highlighting the diagnostic features that distinguish categories. This comparison process is most valuable when categories are perceptually similar (low discriminability) because learners must actively work to identify distinguishing features. When categories are already clearly distinct (high discriminability), blocking is sufficient and interleaving adds little or nothing.

**Secondary mechanism — Desirable difficulties**: Interleaving increases cognitive load during study (more errors, slower processing), which paradoxically strengthens later retrieval. Analogous to the spacing effect — worse during learning, better on test.

**Key moderators (ranked by effect on practical recommendations):**

1. **Material type / category structure**:
   - Paintings: large benefit (g=0.67) — categories look similar but have distinctive styles
   - Math problem types: moderate benefit (g=0.34) — different solution procedures need discrimination
   - Words/vocabulary: blocking is better (g=−0.39) — vocabulary items within a semantic category benefit from clustering, not comparison
   - Mechanism: interleaving helps when discrimination is the learning goal; blocking helps when association is the goal

2. **Category discriminability** (separate from material type):
   - When categories have overlapping surface features → interleaving helps more
   - When categories are clearly distinct → interleaving advantage shrinks

3. **Test delay**:
   - Interleaving advantage grows with longer retention intervals
   - Immediate testing underestimates long-term interleaving benefits

4. **Feedback during study**:
   - Feedback during interleaved practice moderates but does not eliminate the effect; corrective feedback during interleaving appears to reduce the overall advantage slightly (less desirable difficulty)

5. **Age/population** (moderator analysis):
   - Effect consistent across children and adults, though adults show somewhat larger effects — not a major moderator

6. **Number of categories** (within a session):
   - More categories in interleaved set → generally larger effect; more opportunity for contrastive comparison

## Study Design Details

- **Intervention**: Interleaved vs. blocked study of category exemplars (paintings, math problems, words, birds, etc.)
- **Control/Comparison**: Blocked practice (all items from one category before moving to next)
- **Follow-up period**: Ranged from immediate to several weeks; delayed test analyses separated from immediate
- **Analysis model**: Random-effects meta-analysis; moderator analyses via mixed-effects regression
- **Attrition**: Not applicable (experimental studies)
- **Blinding**: Standard for lab experiments; not a quality concern here
- **Inclusion criteria**: Studies comparing interleaved vs. blocked inductive learning with a test phase; category learning paradigm (not rote memorization)

---

## Limitations

- **Stated limitations**:
  - Heterogeneity is high (I²=68.4%) and not fully explained even after moderator analyses — unknown moderators remain
  - Material type categories (paintings, words, math) are confounded with other factors (e.g., number of categories, session length); cannot fully isolate the effect of similarity per se
  - Most studies are laboratory experiments with undergraduates; limited classroom or real-world data
  - Operationalization of "discriminability" varies across studies and was not always measured directly
  - Relatively small k for some material type subgroups (e.g., words: k=8); subgroup estimates have wide CIs

- **Unstated/obvious limitations**:
  - Predominantly WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) populations — generalizability to other populations untested
  - Almost no studies of interleaving in authentic self-directed learning contexts (most are researcher-controlled lab protocols)
  - The "words" finding (blocking better) contradicts intuition from vocabulary flashcard apps; the studies may not reflect how vocabulary is actually learned in practice
  - Effect of interleaving on motivation and study enjoyment not measured — learners often find interleaving more frustrating; adherence in real-world contexts may be lower

---

## Population Scope Assessment

- **Population**: Predominantly undergraduate/college students in lab settings; some children (school-age); mostly Western
- **Relevance to casual adult behavior change**: Medium — the mechanism (interleaved practice for skill/concept learning) is directly relevant for learning features in apps and guidebooks, but the specificity of the material-type moderator matters enormously for any recommendation
- **Red flags**:
  - The words/vocabulary finding (g=−0.39, blocking better) means interleaving is actively counterproductive for common use cases like foreign language vocab or flashcard-style memorization
  - Almost all studies are researcher-controlled, short-duration lab protocols — real-world self-study contexts involve much longer timescales and no experimenter oversight

---

## Conclusions

Interleaving produces an overall moderate advantage over blocking (g=0.42), but this headline masks critical heterogeneity. The benefit of interleaving depends entirely on what is being learned: it is most effective for categories that are perceptually similar but structurally distinct (paintings, math problem types), and it actively harms learning for vocabulary and word-level tasks where blocking is superior (g=−0.39). The unifying explanation is the discriminative contrast hypothesis — interleaving helps when comparison across categories is the learning goal, not when associative encoding within a category is the goal. Practitioners should not apply interleaving universally; the learning objective and material structure must guide the choice.

## Extraction Notes

**Critical scope warning for NotionRPG guidebook:** This meta-analysis is the definitive source confirming that interleaving is NOT a universal strategy. The three material-type subgroups tell three different stories:

- Paintings (g=0.67): Strong evidence for interleaving. Useful for pattern recognition, aesthetic discrimination, style identification.
- Math problems (g=0.34): Moderate evidence. Useful for problem-type identification tasks (identifying which formula/method applies).
- Words/vocabulary (g=−0.39): Do NOT recommend interleaving. Blocking (themed study sessions) is better for vocabulary, definitions, facts.

**Implication for learning-science guidebook chapter**: The standard advice to "interleave your practice" needs a hard caveat. For flashcard-style vocabulary learning (the most common self-study use case for Notion users), blocking within semantic categories is supported by this meta. Interleaving is best framed as a strategy for discrimination tasks — learning to tell similar things apart — not for rote association tasks.

**Conflict check**: The overall g=0.42 is consistent with narrative reviews, but the words subgroup finding (g=−0.39) should be flagged if any other source in the inventory recommends interleaving for vocabulary or language learning without this caveat.
